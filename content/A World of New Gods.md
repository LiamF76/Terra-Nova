## A World of New Gods

---

When Melantha Celestia-Shield, the God Mother, brought back into the world divines that had been dead since nearly the dawn of life, the world was forever altered. These effects, maybe to an unsettling level, are of universal importance.

The sky was alight, as if the sun itself had been burst and spread above Terra-Nova, almost blinding, and from the Oakenheart the for of tens of Gods ascended from their prison within the bowels of Khal Daraan, back to their rightful place. It was a symphony of divinity, and a moment of pure revelry, an event and display of glory not seen since Melantha, and never to be seen again.

The glory, however, was fleeting, for the signs of those Gods was quiet, and whispered. Melantha received her fame, her own worship, and the gratitude of the world, of which she was well deserving. But in the years after, when worship of the new Gods had come, and their communities established, a startling realization came about. An order had been struck between mortals and Gods, an understanding of the relationship, and the players. Adding new Gods, was quite the disruption. More startling than that though, was the quiet.

Their presence did not press upon the world as it once had. Omens faded. Prophecy collapsed into silence. Oracles across Terra‑Nova lost their sight in the same generation, not to blindness, but to absence. Dreams went unvisited. The heavens no longer answered uninvited. The only communication that remained was between the Divine Champions and their benevolent lords. But why? Surely now there were enough Gods to defend themselves, enough divine energy and heroes of good to sway the balance back in their own favor?

Divine magic did not vanish. It concentrated. Where once Gods guided nations, they now spoke only to the few who could bear the weight of their attention. Mortal reliance shifted decisively and dangerously: not to the Gods themselves, but to [[Divine Champions]]. Chosen hands, chosen voices, intermediaries between an attentive silence and a frightened world. Champions became judges, generals, saints, and schematics for morality. Where divine will once flowed outward, it now traveled through individuals, and individuals, unlike Gods, could be broken.

Why did the Gods grow quiet at the moment of their greatest strength?

The answer lies beneath the world.

---

When Khal Daraan awakened, and did bargain with the God Mother at the end of the world, he agreed once again to rest, but not to sleep. 

The Gods do not forsake their mortal worshippers, but they are afraid. They fear not the champions of evil, no Arkonil or dragon of the Triumvirate, they fear Destruction itself, Khal Daraan, resting, and imprisoned within the world, but awake all the same.

To speak loudly is to be noticed. To exert dominion is to invite focus. To repeat the excesses of the old divine age is to hasten the Day of a Thousand Dawns.

So the Gods choose restraint.

The world now exists in a strange balance, both richer in divinity and poorer in certainty. Worship is stronger, answers are fewer, and hope is carried not by miracles, but by people who must act without hearing the voice they serve.

Khal Daraan waits. The Gods endure. And the champions walk forward, bearing a faith heavier than ever before.

Was this bargain worth it? To buy a few years, be they hundreds or thousands, before the end of the world? Perhaps, perhaps not. Only time will tell, and the tower is always ticking...
