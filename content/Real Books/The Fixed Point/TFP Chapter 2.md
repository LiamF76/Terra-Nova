## Chapter 2: The First Mistake

I woke the next day with dirt in my mouth. Not metaphorical dirt—actual, gritty, mineral‑rich soil packed between my teeth like I’d tried to eat the planet on the way down. I spat, rolled onto my back, and stared up at the sky. A sky I had helped make. A sky Shao Daraan had abandoned me under.

The bastard had dropped me just far enough away from my mountain that I had to walk for more than a day. The longing for Lisa came first. Then the grief. Then the cold, hollow realization that I was alone. I had not seen Shao make intelligent life, much had been done so quickly I could not comprehend it. I was in an alien world, entirely alone.

I washed my mouth in a clear river, yet to be tainted by life, for there was nothing in sight but myself. As the cool water ran over my hands, I splashed it against my skin, feeling it cleanse me, enjoying a sensation that had been taken for that long year.

I watched the creek flow, lazily moving down the slope from my mountain. If I was to survive in what would no doubt be a chaotic young world, full of dangers and monsters that could easily kill me, I would need my magic. I reached for it instinctively, pulling the notes of music that make up mana, the strings that tie the present to the past, and the past to the future, but nothing would move.

Where once time itself leapt eagerly to my call, there was no response. I could not read the music of this world, but luckily I could still hear it. I just had to sing loud enough to overpower it. I shoulder and pulled with all my might, sending a shockwave through my nervous system. It takes my effort, every ounce, but I pull the stream backwards, reversing time for the little flow of liquid, before it rebounds back to its original state.

The triumph lasted all of three heartbeats. That was enough for me.

The river snapped back into its proper flow with a slap of water against stone, and the recoil nearly knocked me flat. My vision blurred. My fingers tingled. My skull rang like a cracked bell. Whatever I’d done, it had cost me more than it should have. Time magic was supposed to be effortless—my birthright, my craft, my oldest companion.

Now it felt like trying to lift a mountain with my teeth.

The air above the river quivered—just slightly at first, like a breath held too long. Then the quiver deepened into a tremor, a vibration that hummed through the stones beneath my feet. The light bent. The shadows twisted. The very fabric of the newborn world shuddered.

It was as if something had been napping. Something was waking.

A being, a single echo of light, warm and tiny, hovered before me. It reminded me of the stars that I had seen move around as Daraan called them to him. He had named them his children, the stewards and gods of this world.

It darted around me, phasing through my head as I stumbled backward in alarm.

It paused in front of me, barely an inch from my face, vibrating suddenly, and stopping. Calming.

"What am I?" It asked, in a strangely familiar voice, pitched and feminine, Lisa's voice.

"Lisa...?" I asked desperately, feigning hope that she had made it to me.

"Is that my name? I was... I wasn't. Then you felt something, I was that feeling. I still I am suppose. Are you my creator?" It paused its barrage of questions, its light forming into a face, like that of my daughter. "Are you my father?"

My anger got the better of me, and my hand comes flying upwards, but I do not have the heart to finish the strike. "Please. You must stop that. The voice... and a different face. Be something else."

"I am. I feel something bad now." It mutters as its voice oscillates between other frequencies.

"That is regret I would guess. You did something that hurt me. You didn't know. I'm sorry too." I murmur, dusting my knees and standing.

"Picture something else! I can be that!" It has settled on a strange voice, still like that of a woman, but deeper, a hiss to it, almost serpentine. I picture a fitting form. One from a storybook, a wolf-like serpent dragon from one of Lisa's favorites. 

It changes almost with the first image in my head, though it is demonstrably smaller. Just tiny enough to fit in my hand, like it was scaled to the picture in the book. Almost cute.

"Well done then." I compliment offhandedly. "So what are you?"

Its head tilts like a puppy. "I don't know. What are you? Perhaps I am the same. You did make me after all."

I chuckle, taking the little thing in my hands and placing it on my shoulder. "No I did not. And I doubt we are the same. I am a human, a wizard. I specialize in Chronurgy, time magics. You are some kind of spirit."

"Oh, are spirits good? Humans seem good. And what is magic?" It pesters.

I begin walking toward my mountain, better company that I had been keeping prior I supposed.

"I have met spirits before, yes, I believe you are good, if that is what you are. AS for humans... no we are not good. Most are quite bad. Evil. We are very very dangerous." I say, a small grin touching the corner of my lips as I remember the power I had once possessed, a master of temporality.

"Oh... are you bad, Kairos?" It posits to me. 

I answer honestly. "I don't know yet. But according to your creator, I will be."

I watch my little dragon curl in excitement, more interested in the comment about its creator than my supposed evil nature. "You know my creator! I knew it! Will we get to meet them?"

I bend at the hip and pick up a branch, the dragon falls from my shoulder, flapping its 4 wings and hovering beside me like a flying snake. Its teeth are like tiny razors.

It was a sturdy branch, no doubt fallen in a storm. I snap it in two, making myself a good walking stick. And remembering the words of my old master, though his name... his face... they escape me, 'A wizard is no good to anyone without his staff'.

I turn to face the spirit. The smile is gone, replaced by a stern look of determination as I plant the staff into the earth. Our eyes meet, those of a scorned man, and an innocent mote of life.

"Yes. We will meet your creator. You will see exactly what kind of being it is. And we will give it what it deserves. Together." I raise my staff and point to my mountain. "First we must go there, and find what God has left for us."

---

Another of the lies told by Shao. That is the only thought echoing in my head as I tumble behind a boulder. This is not my mountain, he gave it to a fucking bear.

A great beast of brown fur and massive claws. Just like my world. My world... what world had it been? Where had I come from? Why did Shao bring me here?

There was a pillar of sunlight, a portal, the waiting arms of a woman who loves me... everything had been so clear, a sacrifice made not of nobility but of exhaustion...

"KAIROS!" Something sharp hits my cheek, my mental tangent ends and I see the little dragon floating in front of me.

"Did you actually just slap me?" I as in complete disbelief. Created life, a magical spirit in a fledgling world, and it slapped me.

I am ignored. "Move!" It screams as a road shoots out from the maw of the bear, who has mounted my hiding boulder and is swinging its claws down on me.

I roll to the side, but I am slow. My years are well beyond my 40s, and I cannot dodge like a younger mage. It tears through my left shoulder, scraping bone and leaving me bloody. I cry out in agony, my dragon watches, its eyes are studying me, it has never seen pain before.

Is this the first act of violence in this place? A bear ripping me apart? Strange, but this was to be my mountain, a lying shit-eating god will not take it from me, nor will this bear.

I didn’t rewind the wound. I couldn’t. What I managed was smaller, crueler: I slowed the moment the claw met flesh. The bear’s strike arrived like a film dragged through syrup; the sound of tearing stretched into a long, thin scream. In that thinness I found leverage. I spun, used the bear’s momentum against it, and shoved a jagged boulder into its path. The creature crashed, stunned, more angry than hurt.

It rose, eyes black with the simple, animal certainty of hunger. I met that certainty with something older than spells, strategy. I baited it, led it toward the river where the banks were slick and the ground gave. The beast slipped again, this time into a shallow gorge I’d noticed on the way down. It thrashed, trapped by its own weight and the new geography the world had only just learned to hold.

The beast was immobile for now, and quite angry about it. I collapsed to one knee, the spirit flew to my side, its breath was cool like ice, and soothing. As it exhaled my shoulder began to seal, the wound retreated, turning from a surely lethal gash into an ugly bruise.

Healing magic... interesting, and useful.

I pushed myself up on one elbow and spat a mouthful of river water. The bear lay a few yards away, chest heaving, eyes half closed in a stupor that was more confusion than defeat. The spirit hovered above its flank, a pale smear of light that pulsed with each shallow breath the beast took. It had no name yet. Names matter. Names make things keep their shape.

I turned my gaze downwards and spotted a sharp bit of rubble, picked it up, and trudged towards the bear, kneeling beside the beast. "You saved me spirit. Life is a great gift, so I will give you one in return. Shao said that names were meaningless, but I value them quite highly. I am going to give you a name."

Its eyes sparkled and its ears perked up as it listened.

Names. One binding it to the world, one with meaning, one with strength, one I understood. "You will be called Temporial. You are the Dragon of Time, because once I have my magic back, I am going to teach it to you."

Its eyes brightened at the sound of the name, as if a key had turned somewhere inside it. "Temporial," I said again, tasting the syllables. The name sat on the air and the thing drank it like rain.

I held aloft the sharp stone. "Now then, you have given me life Temporial. Only one thing can pay for life. Death. Only death can pay for life."

"You are going to hurt the bear?" It asks with the innocence of a child, no, even worse. The innocence of something that has never seen death, in a world where nothing has ever died.

"I am going to end its hurting, forever." I bring down the stone on the bear's skull, it makes a hollow and brutal crunch, blood and brains cover me in their gore. I must look a nightmare. The first killer.
