
The sun shone through the store's front windows warmly this morning, forcing Ivar to hold his book at an uncomfortable angle to prevent a glare on the pages. The Legend of Lazarus, an epic tale of the Demi-God king, and a great hero of Estor in the ancient times. Ivar had finally gotten his hands on a copy from a traveling merchant, a Dark-Elf from the far away desert of Drok Shah, but he had managed to track the book down, and nothing could get it out of his hands. The bright rays flickered off his dark hair, reflecting across the shelves of assorted and seemingly random items.
As he scanned the last few pages intensely, completely immersed in his reading as he leaned backwards on his stool, chewing his fingernails as his friends so often told him not to, a shrill voice shot across the counter, "You put that down and work!"

The crotchety old owner of Milton's General Goods, Milton, and Ivar's begrudging employer stared at him with that evil eye of his. His dirty shirt and boots tracking mud into the store as he made his way up the stairs to his apartment, "You gonna wise up soon? I suggest you sweep that up and lock up before you go, already five o'clock. I heard that mercenary is back in town, maybe you'll be better with a stick today than you are at selling axe handles."

Ivar chuckled, Milton was a cranky old timer, but he gave him a job, which was more than he could say for the rest of the village. He didn't hold it against them though, orphans are never truly part of anyone's family, and considering the circumstances of his arrival here, as he had been told them, he understood why some of they disliked them. He cared not. His friends, and their families for the most part embraced him, and Bajurn made sure to bring him gifts and stories from his adventures, what more could he ask for? Better to be disliked and safe, fed, and housed, than anywhere else in the wilds of Estor.

The lord of the area was a Fertan... Ferman, Ferran that was is. Good lord as far as the common folk in here in Rookswood could tell. He kept bandits at bay, low taxes, bit hard on crime but that's how you get to peace Ivar supposed. Ivar had heard stories about the other territories, raids, goblins, monsters of all sorts cropping up. Bajurn had said in his last letter that it was nothing to worry about but there was an anxiousness Ivar couldn't shake... best to keep your head down, that's what Milton says. 

As Ivar finished his mindless sweeping, leaving some clearly unfinished areas, he shouted up to Milton that he was done, headed home, and would see him tomorrow for work!

Ivar left the shop for the last time that day, Milton didn't even hear him go. 

As Ivar walked down the streets, his bag slung over his shoulder full of books he had finished, one tumbled out of his pack. A cover painted with fire, a rare and valuable kind of book for a small town like this, "The Battle of Whitestone and the Red King's Rise", Ivar mumbled. One of his first books, and his least favorite. He held no hate for the story, the King was a good ruler, kind and charitable by all accounts, but he disliked the message. Adrithyr, the king, had rebelled against his evil and manipulative father, and ended killing him and stealing the throne from his older brother and the rightful heir, Calix II. Ivar liked the battles, the prologue about Adrithyr's love for his beloved, but he hated the message. What, if I knew my father, could possibly drive me to kill him, he wondered.
He picked up his book and dusted his knees continuing his journey home to get his sparring sword. Had I known my father, I would do everything to be by his side, a parents love, what a beautiful thing, a thing he had wished for quietly and privately his whole life. That is something I would never throw away or give up on, no matter what happened.

He whistled as he walked, mimicking the birdsong of the Greenpeak Thrush, a native bird to the mountain cluster the village was built just north of. As he absent mindedly walked, lost in dreams of greater things to come he felt a solid thud of something striking his chest, and he found himself squarely on his ass. Ivar looked up, his cool brown eyes in the golden hour of this afternoon, and saw two boys, the Huston brothers. They were twins, both a year younger than Ivar, but they were bigger, stronger, and meaner.

"By the light Davis leave me alone! You want to train with Bajurn today? I'll tell him you were taking rocks at me again!" Ivar bluffed, frustrated and in pain as he looked at the solid stone they had struck him in the chest with. As angry and in pain as he was he wouldn't tell on them, he knew the only thing they had to look forward to was working with Bajurn. Being an orphan and never knowing your parents is one thing, but watching your mother fall ill and you father take tot he drink is just as hard. Taking the thing they loved would only make their days worse, and that was something Ivar wouldn't do. 

The pair stepped forward, looming over Ivar, their dirty clothes and faces giving Ivar an urge to back away, but he stood (or sat) his ground. Davis spoke, sneering at the smaller boy, "We are gonna break you today Ivy! Yer little and Bajurn is gonna see us beat you so badly you'll never get to hold a sparring sword again."

Ivar grunted as he stood, facing them, looking up at Davis and his brother Benton, "I'm not going to fight you Davis, or you Benton. I think we are friends, and I'm going to forget you threw that rock at me. Perhaps you could teach me to fight better, and I could teach you to read."
That was the olive branch, the offer of exchange, Ivar's meek attempt at diplomacy against an enemy whom he stood no chance with in combat.

Davis looked at him almost confused, while his brothers eyes widened, almost excited by the opportunity. They scurried backwards into a two man huddle excitedly giggling, "DEAL!" Shouted Davis as his brother bounced up and down excitedly.

Ivar laughed and smiled, opening his pack and pulling out some books. They're excited, that's good he thought to himself, they just needed something good in all that bad, that's all anyone needs. Ivar handed them a book, the Red King's Rise. They snatched it excitedly, but Ivar held tightly and spoke sternly, "You have to take care of this. Books are fragile and you must be gentle. If you treat the book right it will treat you right and there is a lot to learn from them. This is the story of King Adrithyr, our king. It's not my favorite, so I'll let you borrow it and we can start learning."

Benton quietly whispered to Ivar, "The King, he killed his father, saved us all from his madness right? You like stories huh Ivar... I'd like to be a hero someday, I bet you would like to be a hero like Adrithyr too?"

Ivar recoiled slightly, "No Benton nothing like that. He killed his own dad, he stole his brothers birthright. He lost the woman he loved. I would never want a story like that about me."

The trio embraced, making their way down the dirt road to Bajurn's shack, ready to play hero with swords and words, a night of innocent youthful fun for the young teens. Ivar laughed with them, though his chest still hurt from the rock, but that was the past, and was forgiven. He had learned in a book about the great hero Lazarus that if you cannot forgive, you cannot grow, and he tried to live by that as best he could. An honest and good moral that would be tested for the rest of his life.

"Steel doesn't care if you're tired, Neither does your enemy, GET UP BOY!"

"A warrior’s first weapon isn’t his arms—it’s his feet. Stand right, or don’t stand at all"

"Crying’s fine. Quitting’s not. Wipe your snout and try again."

Ivar had hit the dirt and gotten back up at least twelve times that day. Each time accompanied by a very unhelpful (in Ivar's opinion) comment from Old Bajurn. On the thirteenth time he found himself unable to get up, his back aching as he dragged himself into a sitting position. He spat dust, wiping his shirt and coughing from exhaustion. He knew he needed to get up, but he was tired, it had been almost two hours of practice, and he could barely stay upright on his behind.

A loud thump refocused him, as a gigantic scaled foot appeared in his vision. Bajurn, a beating on the way perhaps? It wouldn't be uncommon, parents of the village were quick with the back of a hand if you were making mischief. The hand came, but no beating, it was open, and it was offered to help him up. Ivar looked up, his hair matted with dust from falling over and over, and met the eyes of the old Dragonborn. The white scaled figure looked down at him, not emotionless, but straight-faced. Bajurn was direct, but kind. He had served in the military, a captain in the army of the local Lord, he had fought alongside and for King Adrithyr, though you would never hear him brag about it.

"You planning on staying in the dirt all day boy? Come then, up up. You lose once when you fall, you lose twice when you don't get back up." Bajurn said as he pulled the boy upwards, patting him on the back and tussling his hair, shaking the dirt out. 

Ivan kept his head bowed, despite the show of kindness, he was ashamed of his mistake, one he continued to make, and he knew he would hear the exact same response. "You were too square again, hips turned, make yourself a smaller target. That's the fifth time today."

Bajurn ran his clawed finger across the boys forehead, lifting up his messy hair to reveal a small cut along the brow. Ivar flinched at the pain, "No son. Remember that. That's what losing feels like. This or worse."

Ivar let a single tear fall from his eye as Bajurn lifted his head, "Don't look down Ivar. Even when you get up the fight isn't over, another swing is coming, right when the world gets you on the ground. Never look down. Remember at this scar. Every scar I’ve got started with a mistake like that. Learn faster than I did. You can be strong kid, if you never stop fighting."

Bajurn smiled, patted Ivar on the back again, before grabbing him by the back of the shirt and tossing him back into the dirt square arena. "Again boys. Ivar seems to think that when blood is drawn these games end. A real fight doesn't start until someone realizes they are going to die. Let's see how hard a bleeding dog bites!"


Ivar made his way home that day with wobbling legs, carrying his stick sword and dreaming of a heroic return from battle. Had he just gone to blows with a dragon, demon, or other great foe? His own fantastic legend spun like thread through a loom in his mind, until he reached the door that is. Before he could reach the handle, the door shot open, sending him toppling onto his back, where he had spend the last hours getting beaten by his sparring opponents. Looking down at Ivar from the door was a strange hooded figure. It was not someone Ivar recognized, and Chian kept a very close eye on every face that entered her tavern, though this face was hidden.

The stranger bent down, getting close to Ivar. His cloak billowed, pitch black, as if it was swallowing the light from inside the tavern. Ivar caught a glimpse of metal, the handle of a blade on the figure's hip, quickly concealed by the cape as the figure dropped to one knee beside the boy. He reached out a hand to Ivar, his pale skin and cold hand startling the boy as he took it. He helped Ivar to his feet, the grip was light, the hands were not rough like a laborer or warrior's, they were fine, like a noble, like a mage.

As their hands touch for that moment, Ivar's vision flickers, he sees something strange, as if he had an ethereal sight. He sees a man, himself, standing on a balcony of a tall black tower. As quickly as it came it had vanished. "Apologies child. I did not intend to knock you over. Allow me to lift you up, young Ivar."

Ivar was stunned by the vision, and barely noted the stranger's unique accent. He sounded highborn, the vowels were sharp and the consonants were crisp. He spoke like a fresh scroll being unfurled. He had heard it before, various visiting lords, some knights that visit the Leaky Log, but nothing like this. He nodded hastily in response, and the man released his grip. Stepping past Ivar the figure almost floated, as if his feet were hovering above the ground. 

Ivar called out as the stranger departed, "Sir, How did you know my name?" He received no answer. The man's shadow moves a half-second too late, dragging behind him like a reluctant afterthought. It flickered and jumped, slightly forward and back, as if it was just out of sync with its creator.

Ivar watched him go, a cloaked figure, clearly magical, who knew his name, and walked out of step with his shadow. Just who in the name of Illuman was that? And why was he at a local bar like the Leaky Log? Ivar shrugged it off for the moment, and picked up his bag, carrying his books and his stick home. He stepped through the doorway into the warm light of the fire, "Welcome back little knight. How many times did you fall today?"

Ivar smiled at Chian, her voice was warmer than the fire, and sticky like honey, every compliment she gave made you feel full like from a homecooked meal. "According to Bajurn, not nearly enough to learn my lesson. But just wait until I get my hands on a real sword. A magical sword. Like in the stories, like the adventurers have. Then nobody will be able to knock me down!"
