
Story about Bazelon Duskfire, a wandering Tiefling warrior. He meets a strange traveler visiting their town, who tells him of a great monster a hero must slay. If Bazelon does his father will surely be proud of him. The monster and traveler are both a god in disguise.

---

Kick's dappled white and brown hair was cool in the autumn wind. Bazelon runs his blood red hand along the horses neck, petting it gently. There was no better sound to Bazelon than the sound of his weapons and armor clanging softly as Kick and himself trotted along the open road.

Unfortunately this was not so open a road, but the smell of fresh pies and the sound of laughter inside the roadside tavern were plenty of compensation. Bazelon hopped down from the saddle, tying Kick off to the hitching post, popping half an apple from the saddle bag into his mount's eager mouth. 

"There's a good girl. You did wonderfully on those muddy roads. Rain won't stop us Kick. Now stay put, and watch out for children, make sure I don't get robbed." Kick whinnied in response as if she knew exactly what her master meant.

Bazelon nodded, smiling in thanks, and turned on his tired traveling boots and making his way up to the tavern. The rusty hinges creaked as the door swung open, but the inside of the tavern was much more lively than the poor old door. Many folks were drinking merrily at long tables and benches, lanterns and a chandeliers lit up the room with warmth and welcoming.

Bazelon took a seat at the end of the bench and was promptly handed a drink. He sipped the ale and remarked on its strong oaky flavor. The barkeep was pleased, as it was brewed at this very tavern. Home grown!

With the small coin purse earned on his travels, Bazelon enjoyed a hearty dinner and shared stories with the locals. he particularly entranced them children with his tales of monsters and magics. He told of his adventures as a Warrior of Kheagon, a wandering fighter who helps those in need, slays monsters and villains, and protects the people of the province.

The night drew on, and Bazelon found himself in a longer conversation with a fellow traveler who had arrived at the in a few weeks prior. They shared stories of strange quests, and adventures to harrowing places. Bazelon was particularly fond of his most outlandish tale, the time he and Kick felled a demon, and Bazelon had taken the fiend's weapon. He had it purified, and enchanted, and no he carried the infernal trident as a mark of his prowess and strength.

The traveler countered with investigative questions, wondering as to why a warrior of such great skill wore cheap clothes and old boots. Bazelon laughed, and countered that the coin was better used to put bread in the mouths of the hungry and books in the hands of the curious than to cloak his Tiefling skin in silks and furs.

Then perhaps, the traveler said, Bazelon should find some furs of his own. Bazelon was deeply interested, both from a sense of growing interest and because he was plenty drunk at this hour.

The suggestion came quickly, that Bazelon could slay a great beast, an Owlbear that had taken home in a nearby grove. Once a holy site dedicated to the nature Goddess, now impossible to enter while the beast called it home. Aye, an Owlbear would make a fine feather coat. Bazelon agreed, a great deed and service it would be to the kind folk of this town. The people said it was a dangerous monster claws sharp, a snapping beak, and it was white as fresh winter snow. Must have traveled to come to a place like this, a backwater inn on an unpaved road. The traveler laughed, commenting that the Owlbear must be on vacation!
