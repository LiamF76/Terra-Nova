
## Time Demands A New Approach


The candle smelled of sea water, salt and sand. It had been quite a long time since he had made a trip to the material plane, research had taken quite the priority. He had forgotten what made the ocean so beautiful, it wasn't the look or the sound, but the smell. Entirely unique, unable to be replicated.

**Replicated**

Things were not going to plan, not in the slightest. Kairos was upset, a polite way to describe his torment over just how failed the replicant plan had gone. Horus didn't quite feel like a failure, though he admitted not living up to his creator's expectations. Replicants were difficult to create, taking one of the core principles of your being, wrapping it in a temporal anchor and creating a new life from it is nothing to scoff at. Horus had been especially difficult, considering Kairos had made him based on his own intelligence. It had set a high bar.

The smell of rain and timber filled his nostrils, as moonlight crept through the tall arched windows.

Horus had done many great things in his life. He had helped construct the Timekeeper's Tower, along with the mechanisms that prevent Temporal anomalies, prevent attacks from other timelines, and protect all of time in general. In his earlier years he had mastered Chronurgy magic to quite a high level, besides Kairos he was the premier expert. He had leveraged his magical talents into teaching, which had been the greatest joy of his life.

Kairos had dispatched him as, 'The God in Glass', a persona crafted to disguise who Horus truly was. he didn't mind the lie, he was happy to be around new people. Apparently on Kairos' home world, men ruled alongside elves, orcs, and all kinds of folk. Terra-Nova was 'far too young', for men to have taken thrones and crowns yet, so the Aelarians ruled. The strange precursor elves, one of which Horus technically was, were like children in many aspects. They were sponges for knowledge, insatiably curious, and frankly, quite a good time to be around.

He thought of those days often, of learning, and teaching. He thought of the freedom he enjoyed, how it had shaped and changed him. Kairos could be poor company, everything was a play, a plotted point and intentional action all part of his grand design. Horus enjoyed a leisure that the original didn't. He liked sitting in a field, listening to a river, and studying magics solely for the purpose of enjoyment.

Unfortunately, the leisure had to end, like all things. Nothing lasts forever. Horus was tasked with a new goal, to find something new with the replicants. Kairos had done all he could, he had no more to draw upon, and thus he could make no more replicants.

Horus rolled his chair away from the desk, it was covered in mindlessly scrawled magical musings. Runes, casting circles, and mana lines all spiraling towards one chaotic center. It was beautiful, but it was if no consequence. He hadn't been able to invent a truly inspiring spell in many years, and had weighed on him.

He stood facing the closest window, a circle between two high bookshelves filled with tomes and magical theory. The storm had picked up, drumming a thousand tiny fingers against the roof and streaking the windows with rivulets that caught the faint candlelight. Horus returned to his desk and leaned back in his chair, eyes half-closed, and let the sound fill the silence Kairos had left him with.

There was something pleasant about the rain, Horus thought as he cast a small firebolt. It careened across the study before igniting a candle along the back shelf. A perfect shot. Two more leapt from his fingertips and lit the other two candles. He did not need the light, it was for the scent. The faint aroma of beeswax and lavender joined the petrichor seeping through the stonework. A simple indulgence, but he had grown fond of them over the years. He swirled his fingers lazily, coaxing the flame higher on one wick, watching shadows stretch and curl like living things across the spines of his endless archives.

The rain struck the windows harder now, a steady cascade that blurred the world beyond into shifting shadows and silver streaks. Horus didn’t mind. He had always found the sound conducive to thinking, a sort of metronome for the mind. There was a chaos, a pattern to it that only those truly attuned to the arcane could feel, the water, the flow of everything, like a river.

"You hear it too, don’t you?" he murmured.

On the arm of his chair, a small creature stirred, a scaled serpentine thing no larger than a housecat, its body a tangle of contradictions. Its scales were white streaked with black, though in the candlelight they almost seemed to ripple and change. Backward-curving horns framed a lupine face, and its tail twitched with a restless energy that made its long, silken fur bristle like a wolf in a storm. Two amber eyes blinked up at him, faintly glowing. His little dragon, no legs, just frontal arms, quite like the ancient Aelarian depictions, perhaps this had been their reference.

Temporial gave a soft chuff, a sound halfway between a purr and the crackle of a fire. *I hear everything. That is only water*, came the voice in his mind, feminine low and smooth, carrying that faintly amused tone the familiar so often wore.

"Only water, " Horus replied, smiling faintly. "Spoken like one who does not understand a world's water cycle, water based evocation magic, or the theory of emergent resonance."

The dragon uncoiled itself with languid grace, arching its back before settling into a circle of fur and scale. Its tail flicked lazily. *This doesn't sound like it has anything to do with Replicants, or Chronurgy, or magic.*

Horus scoffed audibly, "Since when has that mattered? All things are connected. Cain developed his theory for divine binding based on how water cycles through a world: Condensation, transmutation, redistribution. He claimed even the gods are subject to cycles: birth, power, decline, rebirth." Horus leaned back, fingers steepled as he watched a droplet slide down the glass. His face must be warm, as his glasses began to fog up. "It is a simple pattern, yes. But it scales. The micro reflects the macro."

Temporial snorted, a little puff of pale mist curling from its nostrils. It was clearly amused. *Philosophy disguised as theory. Classic Horus. Are you so desperate to be different from the original that you reject doing real research, it's not like your sort can ever run out of time.*

Horus lifted his fingers again, a dancing symphony of mana at his fingertips, flowing between fire and starlight. "Better than theory disguised as certainty. A mistake the original tends to make, one I try to avoid."

*Careful.* Temporial warned, though the amused tone still lingered. *He could be listening to you. He has watchful eyes and ears everywhere.*

"I suspect he is, that would be... quite like him." Horus exhaled softly, watching the candles flicker. "But I’ll risk the offense. A mind like his, brilliant yes, possibly unmatched even, but utterly deaf to the music of small things. He sees time only as a mechanism to be maintained, never as a song to be heard."

*You have a mind like his. One in the same*

Horus scowled, but quickly stilled his face. 

*And can you hear the music?*

Horus tilted his fingers, bending them back and forth from the dancing shadows of the candle. His fingers glimmered with different magic bending light into shadow, until the air hummed faintly with their resonance. "I try." He said finally, his voice softer now, contemplative. "I listen even when the music is faint. Perhaps that is why I am different..."

*Different.* Temporial echoed, unfurling itself and hopping onto his desk, taking a well known position sitting on a stack of books  and staring at the puzzled wizard with its piercing eyes. *Or flawed?*

"Flawed." Horus choked out, his mouth suddenly dry. He grabbed a small glass and sipped the warm water from it, he had let it sit too long. A quick cantrip cooled it, and he sipped again. "That's what he would call me. A replicant fallen so far from the grand design, his destiny, that I have ended up here, alone. I was made to be a mirror of his intelligence, his mental capacity, but mirrors don't reflect what we believe, they reflect what we truly are." He closed his fist, snuffing out the magic in an instant, "And it seems i reflected something kinder than he needed. I don't have the hatred he requires for the task."

Temporial shifted, rolling onto its back and stretching. *Then perhaps you are not a mirror at all, perhaps you are something different.*

Horus scanned the room, eyes settling on the three arched windows to his right once again, the raindrops striking them at irregularly patterned intervals. Beautiful resonance. "Perhaps not. Something different." His words hung in the air like storm clouds, striking his mind with their implication. If he wasn't Kairos he was nothing.

A bolt of lighting struck outside, shattering Horus' quiet contemplation of self in a quite topical way. He reached down and opened a drawer, pulling a heavy fleece blanket from it, and draping it over himself. Temporial's fur bristled as he did. *You are not cold. There is a fire*

He shrugged, shooting another firebolt into the hearth beneath the windows. "Two things can be true." He tried to shrug off the dragon's inquisition, it failed.

*You are afraid.*

He pulled a tome from the stack, causing Temporial to tumble for a moment. It caught itself, and slithered into effortless flight, landing on his shoulder and coiling like a crescent moon along the back of his neck. "And if I was?" He asked loosely, trying to lose himself on page 42 of Krieg's history of Wood (Staves Edition), it was impossibly boring. His fingers tightened on the edges of the tome as though they might squeeze sense back into the glyphs.

*There would be no wrongdoing in fear.*

He ran his earthy eyes over the letters, southern Escarthi pine was exceptional for binding staves given its connection to the Brown Tower, however it was poor for war mages, as it was brittle against elements from the material plane such as silver. He tried to ignore the implication he could be afraid of himself, but Kairos was worthy of a deep terror. "If I ever become quite so terrified that I tremble like a boy in my boots Temporial, you will be first to know."

*You telling me exactly how you feel? Now that is truly something different.* It shifted back and forth on his neck, little spats of static and charge running along his neck hair like lightning.

Something different... a fire shot on in his head. An idea he had been waiting for. "Something different! What if that's what we have been missing all along? We have been trying to replicate Kairos' methods for replicancy, but they come out the same. What if we need something different." He beamed and tossed the useless book back onto the desk, spinning in his chair and standing to face his chalkboard.

*Careful now Horus.* Temporial cautioned him. *Your tone sounds dangerously close to optimism. Last time that happened you made the God of Time into a battery.*

He began scrawling along the board, drawing lines, timelines, paths, lives, each a replicant. "Don't you see? We have been trying to crack a perfect copy, each time using his methods. Planning, inflection points, scripting, its all FALSE. Capturing the essence of his greatness and replicating with precision, but our hands grip too tightly. Too much influence and you get an Emandyr. But perhaps- " His fingers were dancing across the air alongside his writing, magical equations spun about his head as Temporial snapped at them like a hungry dog. "-The flaw is not in our process but in our goal itself!"

Temporial hovered into Horus' vision. The tiny dragon’s horns sparked faintly as it tilted its head. *You're saying we should embrace deviancy? Its killed ten before you, perhaps your hubris has leaked further down into your rational mind that I thought. You want to allow difference?*

"Yes! I want difference, I want deviancy..." His eyes met Temporial as he threw his hands up in a grand crescendo, showing his final equation on the board. A thousand branching lines all beginning at one point and ending at one point, with a hundred possible turns and twists, bone predetermined but all equally possible and unlikely. "Perhaps he is who he is because of everything before the importance. Perhaps the beauty is in choice, allow the seeds of brilliance to grow naturally in the proper soil. Add plenty of water, see I told you it was relevant, and boom! I want to give him freedom."

Temporial hovered closer to the chalkboard, its small lupine face illuminated by the whirling rings of magical equations spinning around Horus’s head. *Plenty of water and good soil. You sound like a gardener. I remember what happened with those roses you planted in the Temporal Plane. Created a whole new specials of age eater plants. What will you do when your precious flower runs wild and out of your control?*

Horus laughed, a spectral mage hand patting Temporial on the head, "That's the point!" The chalk scraped against the slate board as he continued. "Control is the flaw not deviancy! Kairos' replicants worked because they drew upon singular emotions or abilities, anger, intellect, greed, I only deviated because of you. What if we gave this one every opportunity to be their own being? Live thrives in the unpredictable!"

Temporial glided down and stopped as it topped the chalkboard, watching him work with fanatical fervor. *You are dangerously close to preaching. He would kill you for this kind of thinking.*

Horus laughed again as he set down the chalk, his symphony of lines like vines crawling up the lattice of time. "He would kill me for far less."

Temporial’s tail lashed back and forth, sending faint crackles of static through the air, clearly enjoying the pets it received. *You intend to send one mortal, not even mortal, a soulless replicant after him? Top pit one being alone against Kairos, the Fixed Point? You're truly mad Horus.*

Horus stepped back towards his deck, picking at his thick brown beard as he grabbed a pencil, and started to chew on the eraser mindlessly. "I don't intend to pit him against anything. I don't want him to stop Kairos." He bit the eraser fully off and spat it onto the ground, spinning the pencil between his fingers back and forth. "I don't want him to do anything. I'll give him the spark of brilliance, and the rest is his own experience, his freedom. He will be what he will be, savior, tyrant, wizard, or whatever else."

Horus clapped his hands, a symphony of light erupting around him as he bent the time of the chalkboard around itself, speeding it up as it began to spin, the lines fusing together almost looking like turning hands on a clock. "I will simply let them grow. Whatever he chooses will be his own! Whatever it is, will be his own."

Temporial leapt into the air again, perching on the tips of Horus' outstretched fingers. Temporial stared at him for a long moment. *Freedom, the thing he fears most.*

"Exactly!" Horus gave a sad tired smile. "Any coffee left?"

Temporial darted to the steaming pot above the hearth. *It's almost empty and it is quite late, are you not planning on sleeping?*

He shook his head no as he gathered his tomes and started down the hall to the replicant lab. "You should know better by now Temporial!" He called back as he held out his arm for it to land on. "Even us Chronurgist are always running out of time!"
