### Purpose of This Method

The focus is not on planning scenes or outcomes. The focus is on preparing elements that can react to player choices. This prep method exists to:

- Reduce weekly prep time

- Support improvisation

- Keep sessions structured without scripting

- Make prep reusable across sessions


### What a Toy Is

A toy is any element of the game that can act or react on its own. Toys can be NPCs, factions, locations, monsters, or situations. A toy always has:

- A goal

- A way it pursues that goal

- A current problem affecting it


### Universal Toy Template

Use this same template for all toys.

#### A. Goal and Method

This determines behavior in play.

- What does this toy want?

- How does it normally try to get it?


#### B. Performance Hooks (limit to two)

Choose two cues that make this toy easy to run at the table. The goal is instant recall, not detail.

Examples:

- Physical habit or detail

- Speech pattern or verbal habit

- Emotional baseline

- Repeated behavior

#### C. Active Problem

Define something that is currently going wrong. This problem exists whether the players interact with it or not.

Questions to answer:

- What pressure is the toy under right now?

- What happens if nothing interferes?

- How will this situation change over time?


### Using the Template by Category

#### NPCs

Prep only what supports improvisation.

Focus on:

- Clear motivation

- Consistent methods

- One immediate complication


Avoid:

- Writing dialogue

- Planning scenes

- Long backstories


#### Locations

Treat locations as active systems. Prep:

- What the location tends to do (defend, attract, consume, conceal)

- How that shows up (layout, hazards, inhabitants, conditions)

- What is currently unstable or changing


#### Factions

Prep factions as moving parts in the world. Between sessions, update how the faction has acted. Include:

- Primary objective

- Usual tactics

- Current obstacle or pressure


#### Encounters and Monsters

Prep encounters to adapt, not to follow a script. Include:

- Motivation

- One meaningful terrain feature

- One possible change during the encounter


#### What to Exclude from Prep

These elements are decided during play. Do not prep:

- Dialogue

- Scene order

- Planned outcomes

- Player solutions

- Emotional beats


### Campaign-Level Prep (Toy Chest)

#### Initial Setup

This forms the core of the campaign. Prepare:

- 6–10 NPC toys

- 3–5 faction toys

- 2–3 location toys


#### Weekly Prep

Limit weekly prep to answering:

1. Which toys are most likely to interact with the party?

2. How have their problems changed since last session?

3. Is one new toy needed to respond to player actions?


### Session Use

If unsure what happens next during play:

- Identify the relevant toy

- Refer to its goal

- Apply its method under current pressure


### Practice Exercise

Before a session:

1. Choose one existing NPC or location

2. Rewrite it using the toy template

3. Add a current problem

4. Do not plan outcomes


### Ongoing Use

This guide is meant to be referenced briefly during prep. Over time, most of the information should move from notes to memory. The method improves through repetition rather than expansion.
