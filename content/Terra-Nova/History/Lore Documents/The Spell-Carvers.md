## The Spell-Carvers

A year of labor, months of sweat dripped from the brow into the molten metal swirling in the smelter. Weeks of meticulous carving and careful penmanship. The runes had to be perfect, even the tiniest scratch or minor nick along the metal would weaken the magic.

As Goldin held up his creation, a masterwork of artisinl craftsmanship and dedicated study, the blade of the sword glowed faintly orange. He spoke the incantation carved on the blade, "Ashborn, ignite."

The blade erupted into glowing orange and red flames, licking and grasping at every tiny speck of dust on the air around it. It was hungry, for combat, for excitement, and for legend. Sir Abraham would be pleased. A blade to rival the God-Mother, he demanded a fire that burned brighter than Vulcan's heart, and steel sharper than Balthazaar's fangs. He would have neither, but this blade would be enough. He would need bravery, strength, and speed to defeat her. No man can enchant a blade with courage, but this sword could do the rest.

---

Magic. the most potent and desired power in the world, fading like the sun sets at the end of a long summer day. In the eyes of humans it was a drawn out and long passing, but to the longer lived peoples like Elves, magic vanished in the blink of an eye.

Over the course of 400 years, it had nearly died out in the hands of mortals. There were no talented young Wizards attending great academies, or powerful prospects chosen to apprentice for the Wizard's Council. It seemed that as the Gods had been reborn, they took the magic soaked into the bones of the earth with them. It left mortals wanting, but not the Dwarves.

The Dwarves were the first mortals to invent a written language after the Aelarians were eradicated. They are very happy to remind you as such, and even happier to jab at Elves for having beat them to it.

The first Dwarf poets were wizards, they crafted tale and story into word, then into writing, and from writing into magic. Those traditions spread as Dwarves ventured out from their caves, and eventually became known as the Bardic Arts.

Some Dwarves still practice Rune-Carving, though today it is very different from storytelling. Rune-Carving is one of the last methods of enchantment, and skilled Rune-Carvers are vauled for their ability to create items and devices of immense magical power. 

Rune-Carvers can be found in Dwarven kingdoms such as in Xerathos, forging great mining tools to defeat the most dangerous ore elementals and demons of the deep world, or in the wilds. Many choose to roam, citing knowledge of the world as their greatest tool. One such Rune-Carver called the trees and mountains his greatest inspirations. How easy it must been to crave a rune of steady feet and strong shoulders when you have seen the most rooted oaks and deepest tunnels.

If you search Terra-Nova for magic or enchantment, search no further than a Rune-Carver, they may be your best and only chance.
