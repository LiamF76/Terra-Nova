## A Standstill

---

My armor feels heavier than usual. I had it refitted, was perfect around my gut, but I needed a roast and a good ale before this duel. When you fight the Champion of Bravery herself, you need a little liquid courage. 

I pace along the length of the circle, it's not loose sand like that stuff they use in the Blood Circle, desert savages. This is solid earth, dirt and grass. I kneel and dip my hand into the green, feeling its blades along my skin, I try to show respect, hoping the ground beneath my boots will do the same.

For the other men in this tournament it was nothing more than a formality, perhaps less. They knew I would defeat them, I didn't until we started fighting. At first, I am nervous, but I always end up bored. Every fight is a toil, not a test. I know I will win, I just have to wait until it happens.

But not today. Imagine you can see every possible future, choose which action you want in battle knowing its exact consequences, knowing every parry and slash your opponent will make 10 moves in advance. It's euphoric against a great warrior, the thrill of possibility removed, freedom to flow through the field like a river, but she is different.

I see all outcomes, I see all counters, every single step and stride she will make. I see many of her victories, I don't see any of mine.

A breeze rolls over the field as I reach the edge. One of the spectators leans against the fence that marks the wall of my battlefield. "You think you can beat her then young man? That's a tall task, and a tall woman! HA!"

He listens as I walk through the fight in my head.

She starts slowly, she's holding back of course, you hear a story about a boy that can see the future of swords, and you're intrigued, but not convinced. Her strikes are quick, but measured, she's trained the long and short blade, but there is no force in her arm, she wants the fight to continue.

Suppose that's what comes from years of training to kill Demons, you have to learn to cleave through abyssal flesh and still have enough control to hold a glass without shattering it.

I parry everything she throws, and she raises an eyebrow, I piqued her interest. She asks for my name, she knows my name, she wants to rile me. It works.

Her next attack comes towards my ribs, and I point my sword in the opposite direction, leaving my entire side open. He massive sword comes flying towards me but freezes just before shattering every organ in my chest.

Why did I stop? Because I knew she would too. We continue as she laughs, and my ability to showboat dies as she begins to take the battle with nearly half her focus.

We duel, perfectly matched, her strength is beyond reason, but my prediction is unmatched. She does nothing I cannot stop, and our brows become wet with effort. It goes on for hours, and eventually we both drop our blades, leaping backwards and acknowledging our even match.

She commends me and offers a draw. I accept, knowing if this goes on I will lose the battle of stamina, and the battle of skills.

She asks my name again. I give it to her, and I ask hers. She laughs, and a man in a winged helmet trips carrying two glasses of lemonade behind her.

---

My rambling to the poor man foolish enough to talk to me before a duel is cut short when she actually steps onto the field.

She's even taller than I had imagined, and twice as beautiful. Striking, like the pale light of the North Star. She is a mountain, one I would gladly climb, were it not for her husband of course.

"Well met." She says plainly. "Ready?"

I nod, knowing neither of us will walk away from this feeling let down.

"I am Lady. A pleasure to duel the God-Mother."

She scoffs, "Usually the phrase is a pleasure to meet the God-Mother."

"I know what I said." I pace away to take my place. Her laughter fills the air before our swords begin to cut it to pieces.
