A series of poems and readings by Lazarus of Praetoria.

## War

Beneath the Nova-Magnus, all runs red. All that lives bleeds, and all that bleeds screams. 
I have seen men fall from towers and castles, thrones shatter and crowns be stolen.
I wish not to see the screaming, but the laughter. The sound of a town months before I arrive.
Before my legions there is hope, after there is suffering, and during there is the blood.

All that we do in life, echoes in the beyond, and in kind it returns to us in the afterlife.
Deal in death and you will be dealt death in turn, and so is true for all peoples.
In the eyes of Daraan I have seen those who deal in love be given love, and those who heal others be healed.

I shall wait for Erebos and his reaping. When he takes me I will be ready to see, what I have sown across Terra-Nova.


## Power

The scepter carries weight like a gilded chain. It hangs upon you, and the stronger you are the lighter it feels, but it still weighs the same.
The stones a boy and a man pick up carry the same heft, one can simply hold it longer before he falls.
I have carried more stones than a man should see, and I have put down more than any man hereafter will carry. I desire no more gilded chains, red fabric seats, or ornate crowns.

And as I have said, they all weigh the same. I am strong, so I carry it longer, easier, but eventually we all break under the weight. All who hold the reigns of an empire eventually become corrupt. I have lasted too long. 

I put down my scepter, and walk into the unknown, and I find more scepters. Perhaps this land is cursed not with evil but with power, everywhere you go, only waiting to b snatched up and devoured.
