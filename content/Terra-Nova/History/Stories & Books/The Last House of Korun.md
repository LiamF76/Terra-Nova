Once long ago, there was a young half elf man living in the town of Korun, a small hamlet in the deserts of Drok Shah. He worked as a carpenter and builder, but calling it work was a stretch. He was lazy and barely lifted finger whenever anyone asked him to work, he was a total freeloader. 
One day the whole village came together to ask him to just do some work, the easiest job they could think of. There was an old shed built around a well, a well that had long run dry in the desert. The shed was old, the nails were rusted and the boards were falling to pieces. The people knew it was dangerous, and all it had to do was be torn down so none of the village children would get hurt there.
The man, wanting the people to leave him alone so he could continue to laze around, promised to take care of it tomorrow, which overjoyed the people. They of course made him swear to Aestus, one of their most important deities, that he would. He did it with no hesitation, and no intention to actually do the work. 
He delayed the next day, promising to do it tomorrow, and again, and again. The man continued to make promises and break them, until one morning a pair of young children were playing in the village. They ignored their parents waring of danger in the shed, and ventured inside. The people went searching after the children were noticed to be missing, days went by as they looked. No sign of them was found until the young man finally decided to take a look at the shed he was supposed to tear down, and he noticed the smell.
The people blamed the young man, because he had constantly failed to fulfill his promise, and he was jailed for his part in the passing of the two children. Aestus, the God of Promise, took notice of the situation, and decided to give the young man a punishment he would never forget. The great metallic dragon god descended from the skies of his father Aethor, and lay waste to the town. He burned down the houses, the shops, and the farms. Everything burned under the dragons fire, and in the waste the young man wandered. He walked through the ruins and saw everything he knew destroyed, except for one building still standing, the shed above the well.
It became known as the last house of Korun, and the young man learned to never break a promise again.
