## Chapter 1: 

The astral wind howled through the peak of the high tower. The pure marble of the walls remained untouched, but the chimes rang out all the same. I held my hand up in front of the massive slits carved into the stone, feeling the magical air cascade over my skin as it flowed through the structure. I hung my legs over the edge while the lace of my long dress flickered gold and white, shining like the sun itself. I had woven it through magic threads, coating the regular fibers in gold and the enchanted ones in gemstone shavings.

The music was strange, long echoing notes. To most it brought nothing, but to me it carried a beautiful, melancholic sadness. It was the love I felt for a Father I had never known, a Father everyone else had enjoyed. The song was depressing, honestly, but I had earned that pain, and I wanted to share the tower’s dread.

"Astarielle!" A shrill voice called out to me. No doubt Mother had sent Auriga to scold me and drag me down from my hideout. I had requested the Songtower specifically as my home when I finally reached the age to leave Mother’s suite. She knew I wanted it—the day the teachers told me it was Father’s design, it had to be mine. I wasn’t one for material things, not like Auriga. She loved pageantry, courting the fine sons of the Gods, commissioning exotic garments, and adorning herself with the rarest gemstones.

“Astarielle!” Auriga shouted again, now visible on a ledge thirty feet beneath me, her moonlight‑white hair flitting in the wind. I giggled as I began to scale down the spire of the Songtower. Its smooth surface was almost impossible to grip, but with my magic I didn’t need to. I saw Auriga watching me, and I decided a game was in order. Mother always said Father had been a trickster. Grandfather said his kind were born liars.

I feigned a slip, letting my hand fall away from the wall. I screamed—half‑heartedly, but enough. Auriga looked terrified as I began to fall. My dress billowed in the wind, catching beneath the spire and battering me sideways. Auriga leaned over the railing, reaching out, and I caught her hand—though I was no longer falling. I cast flight, slowing my momentum on purpose.

My bare feet drifted down as if landing on an invisible floor, and I hovered there, still holding her hand, before floating onto the balcony, giggling. Her face scrunched for a moment before she steeled her nerves, returning to her unfeeling mask.

"That’s not funny at all. Mortal magics… I always forget how much time you spend on them. Pretending to die is not a joke worth making, Astar. Come on. Mother needs to finish your dress for tomorrow. Please just behave this time."

Her dark skin shimmered in the half‑light, looking almost black. In the day she was the spitting image of Mother, but in the dimming light she became a walking shadow. I kept her hand, holding the cold thing close and placing it on my shoulder as we descended the stairs and entered the halls of my tower.

I knew how much these events meant to Mother, and anything important to Mother was ten times as important to Auriga. She was such a suck up, obsessed with winning favor from our Aunts and Uncles. No doubt hoping to win herself a divine husband like Grandfather wanted for Mother. 

We stride down the sapphire stairs of the central tower side by side, I nod to the servants as we pass. Auriga scowls as I smile at them, nodding and greeting our folk as we go. She never cared for spirits, she called them strange things, creatures more magic than mortal. I never saw much difference between us and them, we were half demon, but I couldn't say that to her, it always made her quite upset. 

One of the butlers, a cloud wreathed in a hooded robe with tiny orbs floating about its neck, hands me a long purple sash. "Oh thank you! I'll make sure to wear it for you tomorrow. Many blessings."

He bows with what I can only hope is a grin showing in those expressive little eyes, and we continue on. Auriga makes a point to examine the sash with distain. I run my hand along the fabric, clearly the butler worked hard on it, it deserves to be shown off tomorrow. I care not what Auriga thinks, I will sneak it on my dress and Mother won't fight me, so long as I promise to attend the Gala.
