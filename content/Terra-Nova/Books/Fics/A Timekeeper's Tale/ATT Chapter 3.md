In the First Age there were few definite truths. Kairos had born witness to the very laws of nature and physics being learned by the Aelarians. Such is the benefit of being the first mortal born into a new world. 'Many thanks to Shao Daraan for that'. He couldn't explain it, but he felt it. The feeling of a condescending 'You're welcome', from the God of Creation echoed in his head.

Though they were considered primitive in their understandings of some things, Aelarians were certainly more successful than any mortal species Kairos had seen in his home world, wherever that was now. They were mages of the very first class, far out pacing Kairos in all understandings save that of time. None would rival him there, as far as these elves were concerned, he was the God of Time. He could have gotten used to that, if he did not have an important mission to attend to. *Justice against the heavens*.

As he proceeded up the steps of the palace, the guards bowed their heads and fell to one knee. One on either side of the stairs falling just before he passed by. Godly treatment, as much as his visage and form were falsehoods, he did not feel like a liar, this treatment was deserved. He was Kairos, the Timekeeper, he built the Tower to direct the events of yesterday and tomorrow. They should kneel for him.

Vaior (Kairos) Was offered magic infused wines, gemstones no doubt worth enough to buy small kingdoms, and plenty of groveling from warriors who sought to be his divine champion. This summed up quite a nice introduction to the Aelarian Kingdom of **Myrrhathal**, known as the "Thousandfold Sanctuary". They were hollow titles, though their palace was beautiful. Marble archways with gold gilding, beautiful artistry across stone tablets, tapestries, and works of grand magic like inverted waterfalls that ran with pink water.

Eventually he was able to reach the throne room, half due to the constant badgering from those who thought he was a benevolent God come to heal all ills and grant riches, and half because he was still trying to figure how to walk while keeping the illusion that he was floating. The disguise was supreme. Not a hit of his wispy grey hair, his young wrinkles, or his humanoid form.

He had taken the visage of a faintly geometric being, a face ever shifting between the angular and smooth, like a constant balance between a sphere and a pyramid. The Gods he had met were either not nearly creative enough, or simplifying their forms for his consumption. If he was going to play God, he would do it correctly, and with proper flair.

Upon the throne sat a wiry young Aelarian, no older than 70 by his looks. The Young King Gwylvein. His people, advisors, knights, mages, and everyone who had ever met him, referred to him as the Unworthy. In Kairos' opinion though, the he was just as worthy as any other King. A trumped up little idiot who thought he was so much better than everyone around him, so much so that he felt he was in charge of said lesser people. So long as Young Gwylvein could recognize Vaior as a God whom he should defer to, there would be no issues.

---

The meeting went about as well as Kairos could have hoped for. Kairos pledged himself, or he pledged Horus, to teach the Aelarians arcane magic. How they had gotten by this long using only the divine was beyond him, but they would advance, and he would use their advances for his weapons. This was a fair bargain in their minds, but in truth it was massively biased towards Kairos' interests.

He took on his true form, releasing the strange geometric mirage around his body. It had become trivial to put up illusions around his form, due to intense practice. He flexed his fingers and legs extensively, pacing across the room he had been given. It was nice to be himself every once in a while, even if it scared the locals.

A meek voice came from the man seated on the lounge chair to his left. "Ah, you possess a mortal form as well your Divinity?" The Knight was brave to make such an observation, especially to a God directly. Luckily he wouldn't have to explain his face to the simpleton.

He continued his pacing across the indoor garden of extensive and beautiful Aelarian foliage. He took in the scents of the flowers, hints of lavender, it reminded him of home. "Exactly right. An astute observation. What was your name again mortal?" He replied without looking up.

He felt the man wince at his tone. Perhaps he had come off more dismissive than he had intended, and the poor little Aelarian was hurt. Kairos sighed and turned, facing him and giving him attention, a gesture of caring for whatever he would say.

His face showed surprise, but composure despite that. "My name is Cain. I'm a magical researcher, I'm very excited to learn from your disciple."

Cain. A brave one, perhaps he could be useful. "Yes, Horus is my most excellent student. I have told him on multiple occasions it was as if he bears my own intelligence, in both the arcane and the temporal. He will guide you well, and your people will see an era of uncontested greatness. Tell me Cain, what do you research specifically here?"

Kairos made his way towards Cain, towering over him in an intended show of their power imbalance. Cain's eyes darted to the floor, perhaps he wasn't so brave after all. "Weapons your holiness. Binding, manifesting, and general destruction. There are many dangers, monsters, dragons, that assail our lands. The Gods cannot prevent every battle, so we must defend ourselves."

Willingness to engage in violence, understanding of the natural order and the need to resist it. Potential.

"Would you be willing to make a weapon for me Cain? I have very specific needs. Say, something that could kill me, or one of my siblings, or perhaps my father?" He spoke effortlessly and with an air of order, like the question was nothing but a rudimentary test he gave to all potential servants.

Cain looked up in horror, his eyes wide with confusion. "You, why in the name of Daraan would you want that? You want a weapon that can kill a god?" His voice trailed off in fear as he raised and lowered his eyes repeatedly, not believing what he had heard.

Kairos could sense his fear, but there was something deeper. An inventor's hunger, an interest, he was willing, he needed to pull the will up to the surface. He reached down and grabbed Cain by the chin, pulling his head up to meet his shining golden eyes. "I want a weapon that can kill *the* God."
