
## CHAPTER 2: 

My ass is numb from landing on it. I roll to the side and gently rub it, groaning in agony from my aching arms. My legs scream as I stand, begging me to fall back onto the ground, but my ass tells it to be quiet, and let me rise. I force myself up again, reaching desperately from my weapon, which clattered on the stone out of my grasp when Jeko hit my wrist and sent me down.

My fingers graze the handle, but it is sent tumbling away from me again. Bajurn's boot knocked it out of my reach as it tumbles into the pool of water. I moan in sadness, the water will make the wood expand, my favorite sword ruined. Bajurn clocks his serpentine tongue. 

"You liked that one didn't you? Well too bloody bad. You fall 10 times in one day, you lose the fucking sword, maybe a new one will help you stay on your feet."

My eyes, like the shadows flickering on the walls, shoot up at Bajurn, the to Jeko, then back down to the floor. I am embarrassed, and I can't hide it.

Training continues despite my shame, and Bajurn spouts his wisdoms each time I strike, each time I fall.

"Steel doesn't care if you're tired, Neither does your enemy, GET UP BOY!"

"A warrior’s first weapon isn’t his arms—it’s his feet. Stand right, or don’t stand at all"

"Crying’s fine. Quitting’s not. Wipe your snout and try again."

His words reach me, and I use them. I get better each time, I am still worse than Jeko.

We take a break, I drink cool water and taste the loaf Brenna sent me, it is a gift to my soul. Brenna's cooking has always been top notch. I needed a comfort, today has not been my day, though when it comes to fighting it never is. The consequences of being training partners with Jeko.

Bajurn sits beside us, handing Jeko his flask, my brother takes a short sip before handing it back. He doesn't offer it to me, he knows I don't drink. 

The old Dragonborn stands behind me, places both hands on my shoulders, I feel the cold of his scales. "Say what you will about Ivar, but he always gets up. Damn I love that about you boy. Always gets up. Blocks with his face! But he never gives up." His hands tighten, a sign of pride in me, not in my skill or lack thereof, but of persistence.

Like swimming against the current of a river.
