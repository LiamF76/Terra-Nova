
## CHAPTER 1: TRUTH, AND WIN ANYWAY

The fog came across the rooftops in a slow wave, like the tides on the ocean. It came before the sun each morning during the fall, I like it better that way, quiet, silver, and slow. The earliest morning is my favorite time of day, nobody out on the roads yet, the world still sleeping, even Brenna. Her snoring is like rolling thunder. 

I pull the heavy fur blanket off, letting the cold air wash over me. A shiver runs over my spine before I roll out of bed. The floorboards are icy cold, and I hop step to step until I can throw on my socks and boots. I pull my cloak on as well, the forest green of the little cape wraps around me, keeping the frigid air at bay.

I bound down the stairs, my boots just a bit too big for me still, next year or soon after I hope they will fit correctly. Once they fit right, they will sooner still be too small, and then I will get a new pair. Might just be my first new anything,but hand-me-down life was better than the have nothing life. Orphans have it good in Rookswood, the village treats every child like their own, and I count myself lucky every day to be one of them.

I throw open the front door, feeling the cold air of outside, laced with the wet earthy smell of grass and dew from the night's cold. Its my morning ritual, walk the fence, check for pawprints, foxes or monsters trying to steal away baby calves or eggs from the hutch. I don't mind the hunters, they are just trying to survive the same as the farm animals we keep. Sometimes I sneak the eating eggs out to leave on the edge of the fence. Brenna would have me over her knee if she found out, but I am very quiet, and very sneaky. She ways I was born a mouse in a boys body.

Sneaking has become harder since turning 13, a coming of age in Rookswood. I have a lot more responsibility now, including the morning check with the general. My hair, blacker than night, falls over my eyes, I blow it upwards and push it back. Brenna loved cutting my hair, it was one of the few moments she allowed herself to touch me. I think she misses physical touch. I like to let it grow longer, I love my hair. I remember the story of Heilong, the Black Dragon. A dark skinned Half-Elf from Drok Shah, the sotry says he got so good with his sword he could make one just from his mind! He bound his hair in thick locks, and grew it until it touched the ground, he would dance and spin, his hair kicking sand from the desert up as he moved, creating a veil of dust as he moved, like an ethereal being.

I come to the door of the hutch and hear a squealing squawk emit from the little chicken sized door. The General, the rooster who lay claim across all the hens in the hutch, the great one I must pass to collect this morning's eggs. My dark brown eyes glitter against the dew of the grass below me.

I prostrate myself, giggling, animals dont have any idea of humility. It clucks softly as it walks down the ramp, stopping in front of me. "OH GREAT GENERAL! ALLOW ME TO COLLECT YOUR MANY WIVES EGGS WITH DEFERENCE AND GRACE!" I am reciting fancy words, stories of the Knights of Iron and ancient Kings of Escarth have me me quite attached to fun formality. Brenna thinks it silly.

I am so still, one of my many sneaky and quiet talents, like a cat stepping acorss ever-creaking boards, without making a sound. He studies for a long moment, before peking the shit out of me. His beak is like a knife of bone, stabbing at my arms and sides. I burst into action, laughing at the sinister little chick and to numb the pain all at once.

"YOU BASTARD! Well I'm gonna eat every little egg now and you get NO SAY!"

---

I trudge back to the house with my basket of heavy brown and white eggs, plenty for the week and to take to market, perhaps I'll even have some spending money for the book shop... no! Don't let your mind race with fantasy and hope, if it comes, be grateful, but if not, work harder tomorrow. I would work for it. My mind dances between dreams of a new tale of the great knight Ferran, the Lord of the Greenpeak Mountains I call home. I shove open the heavy barn door and place the basket beside Brenna, she hardly notices it.

A soft grunt as she turns to reguard me is followed with a warm jab, "He got you good today Sir Ivar." She chuckles as she draws back a hand from beneath the cow she milks and pats me on the shoulder gently. "You walk so quietly when sneaking out to play swords with Bajrun in the wee hours of the morning, perhaps you should sneak into the hutch instead?"

Her knowing eyes pierce mine, aware of my recently developed habit. How did she know? I had been so careful...

I bite the corners of my mouth and twist on the balls of my feet, not fearing retaliation, but ashamed of the lie via omission. "You heard me?"

The cow groans, growing tired of Brenna's touch. "Just a moment more there girl, a moment more. I didn't have to hear you, daft little thing you are. What 13 year old do you know that yawns so much?" She laughs as if my ruse couldn't be more obvious.

"Jeko." My friend.

"HA! Ture, but only because he is sneaking by your side. A pair of lumbering little rats weaving between my fields, off to learn swords with a dragon, in a cave. Foolish. Men who use swords die by swords Ivar."

"Men who die by swords have books about them."

She nods knowing she could not, in her infinte power over me as caretaker, someone who truly cares about me, erase the gfantasy of being a heroic knight from the mind of a little boy.

"Like trying to swim agains tthe current of a river... that's what its like stopping you from getting what you want. Then fine. At least let the sun get to work before you in the future."

She slaps the cow gently on the rear, sending it forward so the next customer can take its place above the tin bucket. I nod slowly, accepting my punishment, but my chin shoots up with wide eyes when I register what she has said. Let the sun get to work first...

"I CAN KEEP TRAINING WITH BAJURN?" I am gleaming, as much as my idea of a stoic unsmiling warrior tries to push forward, nothing could hide my smile today.

She doesn't meet my gaze, working with focus. Brenna always had a hard time showing love, coming from her life as a nursemaid for noble ladies who spent far too little time beside their children. She had developed a great strength in caring for children she kept at arms length, but she had slipped before, I knew she cared.

"You finish all your chores, you do them well, you don't rush. You can learn in the afternoons, and I will send you with food. I imagine that is why you have been having such large breakfasts. But you must make me a promise Ivar. You will never bring a weapon into my house. Never. Swear it on the Sunlight Road."

To swear on the Sunlight Road is to make a promise hinged on your right to the afterlife, to your faith in light and the God of the Sun Illuman himself. His angels were always listening, for truth, and for bravery, but also for oath breakers. Illuman was not my God, though he was chief amongst the pantheon. The leader, the shining and guiding example of all things great men must be. His Hyperion Knights were often the object of the stories I read. 

"I swear on the Sunlight Road, I will not bring a weapon into your house." I swear on something I believe in, the truth of passage to Elysium, walk always in the light. I promise this to Brenna, and I mean it.

I would never need a weapon, I have no enemies. I may be a man one day, but I am not one for enemies and fighting. I made friends even of the angriest boys in Rookswood. I would never have a weapon like my stories, but that would be alright. Someday this will be my farm, and the dreams of grandeur would fade, and that will be alright. Jeko and I will tend it together, and it will be quiet, and happy, and simple.

---

The Dragon's lair is a scary place. Like everything in my books, the stories of the ancient scaled beasts that once scoured and ruled the skies of Terra-Nova, slithering down into dark and dripping caves to hide their covetous treasures. They hide from heroes like me! I am scared, but I press on each afternoon becuase while I am scared, I must be brave. Bajurn chose this place because he knows it scares me. He wants me each day to face a fear, this is one of many.

Jeko's massive shoulder blasts mine from behind, he shoves past me in a great show of force, the difference in our stature has grown much more apparent as we grew older. Numbered together we are 32, a couple of young men seeing their 16th birthdays in just one month. I can scarcely recognize him anymore.

Where I was slender, he was wide. I have good shape about me, the recent years of hard work and training have made me slight and toned, like a great duelist of the Blood Circle Gladiators, or a lanky highborn fencer. Jeko is a wall. He stands a foot taller than I, he is barely over 6ft despite our young age. His arms are like oaks, mine are junipers. Both are trees, sturdy and strong, but his are great thick things that could fight off a storm, lift a great axe, mine swish beside me, long and loose, like an archer.

"Watch where you are going big boy!" I shout after him into the maw of the cavern. 

He laughs, a booming  "Cmon little boy! We have training to do! If you don't hurry I'll take your favorite training sword."

I laugh back, knowing he wouldn't. I know he loves to tease, but I know more so he is my friend, and he knows how angry I get when I have the wrong balance. I am very particular about my armaments. 

I charge forwards after him, shutting my eyes for just a moment to avoid looking at the spiky stones and darkness approaching from the cave as I catch up to Jeko.

Torches guide our way, though there is only one path into the shallow place. The floor of the cave is level save the small pools of water. Bajurn built them for us, obstacles to guide training, no battlefield is empty of threats.

He waits for us at the bottom, Jeko leads the way. Bajurn's white scales glimmer and molt, looking almost gold in the torch's light. His arms are crossed, standing and waiting impatiently. He looks up as our footsteps echo through the cavern. "It is about damned time."

His accent is tough, northern, as he was raised by his people. The Dragonborn, scaly humanoids, a made race, not bestowed upon Terra-Nova as Humans were, or descended from the first people like Elves. Dragonborn were created by Dragons thousands of years ago, made to be servants, their great armies to conquer the world. They failed, and many Dragonborn became free, the rest did so when the last of the dragons died out a few hundred back.

His snout puffs out a burst of ice cold air as I pass, head down in shame. We had slacked in our chore effort. I had been reading, and Jeko had been listening. The autumn was simply too good to waste on ripping weeds all day, felling trees to expand the field had created many a good stump to sit upon. One such stump had been such a joy under our asses that we had sat there for almost an hour, which I was happy to do with Jeko, who I consider my brother.

"Sorry Baj, got caught up working I swear." I say meekly as I pick up my arms, Jeko handing me my favorite wooden sparring sword. I give him a nod.

Bajurn doesn't answer, he knows I'm lying. He doesn't care for lies, lies from the mouth are for cheap men, weak men. Strong men lie with their hands, with their swords. Lies can get you past an enemy's guard, can get you to the soft belly of his flesh and give you opening to stab. Bajurn is not a liar, Bajur taught us to tell the truth, and to win anyway. Thus, he does not respond to my falsehood, he simply watches us take our places and frowns.

He is disappointed in me, I hate it. I spread my feet wide, shoulder width, turning to make myself an even smaller target than Jeko could hope to be. I loosen my body, ready for yet another ass-kicking by Jeko, he's much stronger than I, and he almost always wins. 

"Begin."
