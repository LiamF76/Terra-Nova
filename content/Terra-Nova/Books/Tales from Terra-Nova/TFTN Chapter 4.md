## Letters to the Moon

His desk was laden with letters, 17 in total, each written by his own hand, each sealed with the blue eagle of his own house, and each doomed to wilt there, never sent, never read again by their intended recipient.

The candle beside him guttered and wept wax down its length. He frowned at it, striking a match with awkward fingers. The first broke. The second sputtered out. The third flared to life, and he held it there a moment too long, watching the flame curl dangerously close to his fingertips before touching it to the wick.

"There," Lukas murmured to himself. "A battle well fought." He allowed himself a crooked smile at his small victory.

He had never been good at anything practical, or simple, for that matter. Too many years spent with servants anticipating his every whim, dressing him, feeding him, even opening his blasted letters. It was a habit he was determined to break. He was a prince of Adrithyr, yes, but more importantly, he was a man—he would not be a burden.

The ship rocked violently beneath him, sending the ink bottle skittering across the desk. His hands shot to the wheels of his chair, locking them just in time to keep from rolling backwards. His eyes darted to the window, where the frothing sea churned hundreds of feet below as the airship pitched in Konduell’s Grasp.

"A fair attempt you salty bastard." He muttered, cursing the Eldritch Lord of the Seas. "But not today."

He released the brakes and pulled himself forward again, fingers brushing over the sealed letters. Each one bore her name. Elena Syrell.

As children, they’d written to each other often. He could still remember her spindly handwriting in the margins of books she’d lent him, the smell of ink and lilies lingering on each page. She had been bright—so terribly bright. Even after all these years, her memory felt like moonlight: soft, distant, untouchable.

He had not sent her a letter in almost ten years.

Seeing her at the War Council, Castle Ikaris, had been a thrill beyond his own comprehension. He had barely been able to stop himself from charging towards her and falling to one knee then and there, but his father would have called him a witless whelp, so he restrained. Had it not been for the urging of the Young Wolves adventurers, he probably would have waited a few days to talk, and with all the commotion and murders, he was glad he hadn't.

He had seen no judgment in her eyes at his condition, granted he had mate insurmountable effort to put those concerns to rest. His father had pushed him beyond his limits, and he was proud of what his son accomplished, even for a fleeting moment.

His magical legs, a marvel by most arcane standards, but not they felt second nature. Made of pure blue electricity fields, upheld by his own power, hell they had even given him some quite powerful flight. His physicians and advisors were astonished, but he knew it would never illicit more than a 'well done' from his father.

She asked about the battle, about how he was doing, not as a prince, not as a soldier, but as Lukas. She spoke to him as if he were still the boy who’d stolen tarts from the Lakefort kitchens and argued about constellations with her beneath the ivy arches.

And for a moment, just a breath, he let himself believe he could stay in that light.

But reality had a way of creeping in. The way the council chambers fell silent when he entered. The way even seasoned generals looked at his legs—not with awe, but with caution. And beneath it all, the memory of Adrithyr’s voice booming in his mind like a curse.

"Don’t mistake kindness for invitation, boy. She’s Syrell to the marrow. That House has no love for us. She’ll see the tyrant in your blood before she sees the man."

He’d laughed at the time, bitter and defiant. But now, alone in the quiet hum of his cabin, Lukas felt the weight of his father’s words like a chain about his throat.

The ship jolted again, sending a loose letter fluttering to the floor. He reached for it clumsily, his fingers brushing the seal as he turned it over in his lap.

He whispered into the silence. "Would it be so terrible… to let her know?"

But no answer came. Only the steady thrum of arcane engines and the restless hiss of the sea winds clawing at the hull.

*Lady Elena,*

Do you remember the old ivy arches in the gardens? You once told me the moonlight made them look like the gates to some fairer world. I stayed there long after you left that night, staring up, hoping the gates would open for me too.

You always saw more than the rest of us. More than me. That frightens me sometimes, though I couldn’t tell you why. I think it’s because you see things in people, greatness, goodness that they don’t believe exists in themselves.

I wonder… if you ever looked at me that way?

Yours in thought, 
**Lukas Realmcrest**

*Elena,*

Do you know how it feels to become your father’s greatest disappointment? I do. Every morning when I drag myself out of this cursed chair and try—try—to be a prince again, I hear his voice. *Pathetic. Broken. Unfit for throne or war.*

And yet, when I think of you, I feel almost whole again. Almost.

But I know better. I know you’d never let pity lead you astray, and I cannot—will not—let myself hope for something that would tarnish you by association.

Forgive me for writing. I will burn this tomorrow.

—**L**

*Elena,*

I saw you again today. I swear, I had prepared myself for it. Told myself you’d be colder, more distant after all these years. You weren’t. You smiled at me. Gods, I think it undid something in me I had worked years to lock away.

I wanted to tell you how proud I am of you, how I always knew you’d rise beyond Lakefort’s walls. But I bit my tongue. You looked tired, burdened, and I—selfishly—couldn’t bear to add another weight.

Perhaps next time, I’ll find the courage.

Until then, I remain,
**Lukas**

---

*To the Moonlight Maiden,*

I have written you seventeen letters. This makes eighteen. Not one has left my hands.

You deserve more than a maimed prince with his father’s sins in his shadow. You deserve the world itself, unspoiled and without compromise.

To love you rightly, I think, is to never let you know.

And so, this too will stay sealed. Perhaps one day, when the world no longer needs me, I will find the courage to speak plainly. But for now, I will remain silent.

Watching. Hoping.
**—L.R.**

The throne room was silent but for the sound of rain tapping against the high arched windows. The torches had burned low, their flames flickering in the drafts that snuck through cracked stonework.

Lukas Realmcrest sat slouched upon the throne, not quite as a king should, he supposed, but he was not quite a king. His prosthetic legs hummed faintly, static along their length pulsing in dim, electric blue as they replenished their energy. He tapped a finger against the armrest, lost in thought.

The ruby red crown of Adrithyr, still too large for his liking, rested unceremoniously on the table beside him.

*Father never let the damn thing leave his head.*

The thought soured his mood further.

He didn’t hear the door open at first. He didn’t notice the soft footfalls until a voice, warm and amused, broke the quiet.

"Well Lukas..."

He flinched, bolting upright like a boy caught napping in the library.

"... you've taken to writing me letters again? I counted at least 15, and you've been hiding them away in your room like stashed sweets?"

Elena Syrell leaned casually against the throne room doorframe, her dark eyes gleaming with mischief. She wore no gown tonight, only a traveling cloak still damp from the rain and boots that tracked a faint trail of water across the marble floor.

"Elena?" He said in utter confusion. "I- how- What were you doing in my chambers?"

"Ph well, I figured you'd like a visitor. I know things can't have been easy what with the grim conditions of your coronation." She strode closer, unhurried, calm and collected. "Imagine my delight when I stumbled upon all your letters, addressed to me, bearing the royal seal no less." 

She stopped just short of the dais steps up to the throne. She tilted her head, smiling like the moon peaking out from behind the clouds. "I haven't opened any of them. Yet."

Lukas’s face was aflame. He half-rose from the throne before remembering the absurdity of his reaction. He sank back down, trying to compose himself. "You shouldn't have-"

"Oh I shouldn't have?" Her grin widened. "Lukas Realmcrest, King of Escarth, leaving his most private thoughts out on his desk the the journal of an idle schoolboy? I think I may have done you a favor! Imagine of Firebrand or Kaela had found them. Your advisors are so serious."

"They're not-" He hesitated, gasping for air as the words caught in his throat. "They are not finished."

She arched a brow, waving the stack in front of him. Sealed, "stamped, addressed. How unfinished can they be?"

Lukas felt small, smaller than a boy, like he was shrinking by the second. "They weren't meant for you to read." He said softly.

That quiet pulled some of the teasing from her eyes. She studied him a moment longer, then softened. "I didn’t mean to embarrass you. Truly."

Lukas looked away, fingers tracing the armrest. "You didn’t. Not entirely."

She released them from her grip onto a small table, and sauntered up to him, sitting on the opposite armrest. "This throne doesn't suit you. Its cruel, cold, stone. Like your father. You should get something warmer, nice "

Lukas huffed a dry laugh. "A cushioned throne. That’ll win the lords over."

"It would win me over." Elena smiled faintly, tilting her head to peer at him. "Tell me... how does it feel? Sitting here. King in all but name, while your father still breathes somewhere out there."

Lukas sighed deeply. "It feels wrong, like I am wearing another man's clothes, they are heavy and cold, wet with blood I did not wish spilled." He leaned backwards further, his face pointed upwards to the beautiful carvings and paintings that adorned the ceiling. "My father gave birth to this rebellion. I've read the logs, Kaela's duties, the children they hunted, what he did to Ivar's mother... he's vile. He started all this death when he murdered her, and I have inherited it. I sit upon a blood throne with a  blood-soaked crown."

"Do you hate him for it?" she asked gently.

He pondered a moment with belabored blinks. "I do." It was a painful admission, but honesty was everything. "I don't wish to speak of this anymore. Please."

Elena studied him for a moment, her expression unreadable. Then, with a little sigh of her own, she leaned forward and plucked at the edge of his sleeve. "Good," she said lightly. "You’re far too handsome to ruin with all this brooding."

Lukas fumbled the words on his tongue without any of the royal grace he had been tutored in. "I- well that's, uhm- you see..."

Her finger hit his lips, silencing him as he looked up at her. Shining like the moon, impossibly beautiful, almost alien.

"Don't argue." He nodded absent mindedly. "Just take the compliment Lukas."

"You're impossible." He whispered through her finger, still touched to his lips. It tasted of blueberries, had she eaten his pie?

"That is why you like me."

With that she hopped from the throne. Striding down the steps with grace, Lukas' eyes locked on her hips, the way her dark hair shone and swayed as she moved. "Come along then your Grace!" She beckoned with one finger, not turning back. She reached down in the doorframe as she left, lifting her scabbard and blade to her shoulder. "Let's go to the sparring grounds hm, I will return your sparky ass tot he dirt where it belongs. For old times sake."

Lukas blinked, then huffed out a soft laugh that surprised even him. He wasn’t sure when his heart had started pounding so hard.

"You’re going to regret that," Lukas said as he pushed himself up from the throne. He reached for the long spear resting against the armrest, its polished haft catching the moonlight. "I’m almost a foot taller than you now." He let out a low laugh, rolling his shoulders as he stood to his full height.

Elena turned in the doorway, her dark hair spilling down her back like ink. The moonlight caught the curve of her cheek, and he swore he saw the faintest flicker of a grin as she tossed a glance over her shoulder.

"All that height didn’t seem to help you against Ivar..." she said, her voice lilting like a melody, just sweet enough to mask the blade hidden beneath her words.

"That’s a low blow, Elena." He tried to sound wounded, but the corner of his mouth betrayed him, twitching into a reluctant smile.

"Well, you did say I was short," she shot back smoothly, one hand resting lazily on her sword hilt. "Come on! Let's see if my sword can still reach you all the way up there. What's our score at now? Last I checked it was 7-2 in my favor."

Lukas jogged over, before remembering his abilities, and took to hovering just a few inches off the cold tile floors. "Then I'll have to beat you five times tonight and even it out."
