## A Hunger for Knowledge

The workshop was full with the stench of ash, iron filings, and old oak bark. It sat warm and heavy in his little lungs, a familiar stench that reminded him of his fathers work whenever he smelled it from this day on. Essek sat crisscross on the ground beside a large table, leaned comfortable against one of its sturdy stone legs. His sleeves were rolled up over his bony elbows, as he passionately polished a piece of runeplate armor until it gleamed and he could see his face in its reflection. Every so often he blew the copper dust off and compared, giving half-hearted effort to match his father's well kept example.

"Careful boy." Came a low gravelly voice from over the bench, his father. Harlan Eyewright didn't look up as he commented, focusing entirely on the pure silver band in his hands as he coaxed careful and delicate runes onto its surface, each one glowing faintly blue before settling with arcane calm. The chisel in his hand hummed faintly, each tap releasing another pulse of light before dimming. "The Academy mages won't pay if you scratch it again."

"Yes Papa." Essek answered meekly, but his voice lacked focus, he could barely keep his eyes on his task anymore. His gaze wandered over and over to the doorway, and down the dimly lit hallway beyond it.

Harlan had been lucky to be given leave to work here, even in this old forgotten annex of the Redsong Academy. Students rarely ventured here, which made it perfect for bringing his boy along, no distractions, and it was quiet here. The walls were lined with dusty tomes many issues out of date and rusted implements of almost no use anymore. Some were so ancient they looked more ceremonial than useful. Essek had always held a faint sense of wonder about this place, it had a strange aura, as though the stones remembered days far gone where they had housed more powerful and important activities than this.

Essek shot a glance over the bench at his father, so deeply absorbed in his work he surely wouldn't notice if his son wandered just a bit. The floors of the hall were coated in dust save his father's boot prints, alongside his smaller marks from their entrance this morning. As Essek walked he could feel the strange arcane force, the air still carried echoes of long forgotten incantations. Half the doorways in the hall were warped shut, their sealing glyphs long since extinguished.

One doorway however, was not so silent.

Essek had noticed it hours ago, one door hanging ajar, leading deeper into the annex where a faint red glow hummed, almost calling to him. Essek padded silently along the stones, and slipped through the door without a sound.

---

The chamber beyond was lit by embers clinging to life on wilting candles. Faded white chalk circles and stars marred the floor, their designs strange and foreign to Essek. He pondered them as he nestled against the doorframe, fearing to step into the strange chamber. As he peered over the symbols he noticed a small tome, barely the size of his hand but thick with many yellowed pages. The air in the room was heavy and doused in the scent of iron and herbs.

Something stirred in Essek's heart, he had always loved reading, stories, research, tales of what lies beyond. That tome was special, he could feel it. He dared a single step forward, the bust of dust beneath his old torn boot startled him, but the allure of the tome gave him the courage to continue.

Its cover was cracked leather, etched with a star inside a circle that shined in his eye like moonlight. He wanted it.

He knelt, hesitant at first, then reaching for the little book. His fingers ran over the cover, it was warm to the touch, almost humming to him as he caressed it.

**A Candle in the Dark: Essays on Diabolism**

His lips mouthed the words silently a few times over. Diabolism. He had heard it in whispers before, layered with warnings of expulsion and hushed fears of what might occur. His father said magics like this were chaotic, dangerous, dealing with forces beyond the material plane led only to ruin.

And yet... Essek's fingers tightened around it, and he picked it up. A little voice, his better sense, told him this was a mistake. but he shoved that thought aside as he flipped the book open to its first page.

| For the curious of mind and reckless of heart, study clearly and with intent the workings of the Abyss, and a more learned mind than yours never shall be unearthed.

His eyes drank in the opening lines like he had a thirst built over months.

| Diabolism is a study shrouded in fear, but that fear is unfounded and born of ignorance. May this tome  guide you whether you have been studied in the arcane for years akin to a Grandmaster, or have never cast a spell in your lifetime.

Essek's eyes were wide with awe as he flipped through the pages, dossiers on different demonic beasts, summoning techniques, alchemical uses for different hell-born materials. It was an overwhelmingly useful font of knowledge. You are not withheld to power in this study. Draw the circle, speak a name, and offer a price, anything can be yours. You can be the master of otherworldly forces.

A thrill shot through his chest as he held the book to his heart. This was the thrill he had been searching for.

The sound of the chisel clinking against silver shattered his daze, and he heard his father's voice call out. "Essek? Where'd you wander off to now boy?"

He twisted quickly, shoving the book into his pants pocket. The candles around him flickered in response to his panic. Essek glanced once more at the chalked circle, burning its design into his mind before leaping upwards and running towards the workshop again. "Coming Papa!"

As he pattered back to his father he knew this was not a passing boyhood hobby, this was the beginning of something much larger. This was the day Essek discovered his passion, and it would shape him forever.

---

The Scar sang horribly tonight, like grinding glass against bone. The tear in reality hummed and screamed with a low unending screech. The wind did not howl here, it was demonic laughter riding along the wind like a chariot. it was dry and hot, leaving Essek's mouth stale and desperate for water. There was no water here.

Essek wet his lips from a small glass vial, the red metallic liquid barely keeping the thirst at bay. Blood was the closest thing to clean water here. He dragged himself up a stony ridge, had his hands not hardened from the conditions these stones would surely have torn his skin to ribbons. He clutched his worn satchel as his rune-lamp flickered, it was running low on mana, and he had no more crystals to fuel it.

A cough reminded Essek of his companion, whose dark skin was almost grey beneath the thick layer of ash and dust that had engulfed the both of them. "Still alive then are you?" Essek asked dryly. He did not offer Talinor a hand up the rocks.

"Alive." Talinor rasped as he grabbed Essek's belt, using it to pull himself all the way up the ridge. "And regretting it more very day I spend with you. If you insist on climbing every gods-damned ridge in the Abyss you are welcome to, but I'll let you do it alone or so help me Ishtar..." He trailed off angrily, his pause initiated by the sight from the ridge.

Days of dust storms and blood rain had left them stinking and blind walking through the dark with nothing but Essek's lamp. It was nice to finally see the 'sky', be that as it may, clouds of eyeballs, mist of rust and blood, it was horrifically beautiful, and yet awfully the same as all the rest of this place.

Essek adjusted his cracked spectacles as he looked over the land. For the briefest moment Essek couldn't control his left arm, the fingers felt off, as if there were too many joints, not enough fingers, but it passed quicker than it came. "You would starve alone." He answered in a very matter of fact tone. "We both would."

Talinor spat into the ash. "We are starving anyway." He wiped across his lips with what remained of his white and silver scarf, the symbol of the Ivory Spire Academy. "Face it Eyewright, we aren't surviving this."

---

They walked in silence of hours, keeping their bodies going with meager droplets of blood and tiny scraps of meat. That was one perk of the Abyss Talinor had genuinely thanked Essek for. It was so stale and dry here that all you had to do to dry meat safely was tie it to the top of you pack and wait a few days, made the rations stretch. 

Their feet carried them past twisted pillars, what might have been trees or architecture, Essek couldn't tell, Talinor didn't care. A long sinister shadow crawled in the distance, stalking them just our of their field of vision. It was too large to be human, or humanoid at all. Both men froze.

"Another." Essek muttered, keeping his body as still as he could.

Talinor raised his rail thin arms with excruciating effort, drawing the most basic warding glyph in the air. His dark fingers and blackened fingernails moved with deadly precision, he may only know the simplest cantrips, but he knew them well. He was proud he could still manage to draw one, but it sputtered and faded. "Fucking Abyss. Whatever bleeds through this place is killing my magic. Faster and faster every time."

"Don't use more than you must." Essek pulled him down behind a massive shard of bone. As he did his fingernails shone strangely, black veins spiderwebbing beneath the skin, but the shadows shifted and they disappeared. "They can sense arcane magics."

Talinor spat blood into the black sand. "And what do you suggest, prayers?" Talinor's lips curled. "Save it Redsong. I don't know any. Your type has such strong ties to the Hyperion Church. In Drok Shah we learn very quickly that the sun is no friend."

Essek took a band of demon skin from the corpse they had scavenged days ago, wrapping it around his forearms as some cheap imitation of armor. His pupils bored into Talinor, strangely thin in the darkness of this place, almost vertical for a moment. "Fuck prayer. We study it, observation. Learn its patterns."

"And when it learns *ours*?" Talinor's voice was venomous.

Essek chuckled before it fell into a raking cough. "Just have to learn faster. I would say there's no way some shit-stain demon is smarter than you, but you went to attended the Ivory Spire."

Talinor ran his hand through the sand, letting it fall through his fingertips like raindrops. "Go fuck yourself Redsong."

---

2 more torturous hours. Talinor wouldn't stop breathing so loudly. Essek wanted to wrap a piece of the bloody leather from his wrists around Talinor's neck and silence him forever, or more reasonably, ask him to quiet down. 

The air thickened with the stench of sulfur and old blood first. Always the precursor to demons, the goddamned sulfur. Essek's nostrils turned upwards in a deep demonic huff as the shadow slinked along just out of his sight.

"Behind." He hissed. His broken lenses caught a flash of glowing green eyes in the haze. The pair turned to face it. He could hear a faint flapping sound over the gale of screams and wind. "It flies."

The beast stepped into view, or hovered. It was a beast, its red skinned body covered in bloody pointed spines. It had blood red leathery wings, and in its hands was a crude Abyssal Iron Trident. Its eyes were two gleaming embers of hatred.

**A Spined Devil.**

Essek retreated back behind the shard of bone, leaving Talinor alone facing the beast. "Buy me some time Talinor!" He shouted as he rummaged through his satchel, pulling a pouch of salt and a piece of incense from it.

"Buy you some time? The fuck do you want me to do throw a book at it?" He shouted back as the Spined Devil advanced towards him.

"Two rats under one rock, how delicious." It sneered, drooling a sickly green bile as it licked its lips in anticipation. 

The devil's tail flicked, launching two spines toward Talinor. He rolled to dodge, avoiding one, but the other pierced his leg, leaving him with a deep piercing pain, he bit back a scream. Talinor raised his hands, shaking, as he steadied them for a moment to cast a spell, **Minor Illusion**. The sound of collapsing rock and dusk erupted behind the devil as the illusion conjured a rockslide. The devil turned and fired off a barrage of five spines at the falsehood.

Essek pulled his salt and began to pour, creating a very crude circle. he would need it for the binding. Next he searched for his inkpot, which he found to be cracked, and the ink from it had leaked and gone, it was useless. He tossed it to the side. "Talinor! I need something to draw with, like Ink!"

Another spine flew towards Talinor, which Essek watched fly over his head as the Half-Elf mage leaped behind the bone shard alongside his companion. "You wanna draw? Here." He groaned in agony as he ripped the spine from his leg, still dripping with his blood and tossed it to Essek.

"Thank you." Essek said, and began illustrating binding ritual incantations upon the ground.

Talinor desperately searched for something to fight with, and found it. He snatched up Essek's broken inkpot. and hurled it towards the quickly advancing spined bastard. Mid-arc he snapped his fingers, and his mage hand swatted the pot, accelerating it as the glass shattered and filled the devil's eyes with glass shards. It was momentarily blinded.

Talinor shot a glance back at Essek. "Can you do whatever you're doing faster?"

Essek laughed. "No." His sigils began to glow faintly. He was muttering between Common and Abyssal, each word tearing at his throat as he chanted.

The devil screeched, wiping glass from its eyes. It dove, claws outstretched. Talinor threw himself into its path, swinging his satchel like a club. Books spilled everywhere as the spine devil raked a shallow cut across his chest. Essek looked up from his half-finished circle just in time to see Talinor pinned beneath the creature’s weight.

"No!" He raised a shaking hand, casting a **Mage Hand**, but it wasn’t enough to move the beast, but it nudged a loose rock from the cavern ceiling. The stone fell, slamming into the devil’s wing.

It shrieked, tumbling sideways, giving Talinor just enough time to scramble free. "Are you going to help me Redsong?" He shouted as he wrestled free.

Essek barely heard him. His gaze had locked on the creature’s neck. Around its scaly throat was a collar—iron, etched with runes so faint they seemed almost worn away. But to Essek, who had spent decades buried in abyssal tomes, the pattern was unmistakable.

An ownership glyph. He knew that mark, he had seen it many years ago, in his first Diabolism tome.

"Vexis," he whispered. "Vexis the Thousand Eyes… Lord of Spies… the Whispering Veil."

The devil froze mid-wingbeat. Its ember eyes flickered uncertainly.

"You speak… forbidden things, mortal." The voice lost a hint of its malice, replaced with something colder. "Where did you learn that name?"

Essek’s eyes glittered strangely in the gloom, pupils narrowing to vertical slits. He rose, shaky but resolute, tracing a quick binding circle on the ground with chalk and blood. His voice was low and sharp as a dagger.

"Your collar binds you to the Lord of Spies. By his name, I bind you now."

The circle beneath him pulsed with a dark light.

"By the Thousand Eyes I see you, by the Veil I cloak you, and by the Whisper I silence you. Vel adstringe!"

The spine devil shrieked as luminous chains erupted from the floor, latching onto its limbs and dragging it down. It fought—flames spitting from its maw, spines rattling loose—but the binding sigils crept up the collar, sealing its connection to Vexis.

Talinor stared in disbelief. "Y-you’re invoking a Prince’s name?! Are you mad?"

"Desperate," Essek rasped. "But the theory holds..."

With a final word of power, Essek clenched his fist. The collar’s runes flared red, then shattered. The devil’s form twisted violently, compressing inwards as its essence was vacuumed into the binding circle.

What remained was a small, faintly glowing mana crystal, humming softly with infernal energy.

Essek staggered and fell to his knees, clutching the crystal. His hand trembled—not just with exhaustion, but with an odd, unnatural stiffness. He quickly stuffed the crystal into his lantern, and held it high.

"Come Talinor." Essek reached out a hand to his ally. "I have made a dangerous move, and I would like to be elsewhere before the Prince's minions find us, and we meet the Thousand Eyes."

"You and me both."
