The sun hangs low in the evening as we reach Tarnkeep. a few jabs from Towser came my way, but I don't mind. I would rather he pokes fun at me than at Lukas, the Old Man can barely stand his insults. It isn't hard for Towser, he knows how to rile up Petlia, don't even need to speak ill of him, just call him a liar, sets him in a sour mood for the rest of the day.

I have never visited the city before, but Tarnkeep earns its name. The fortress sits up on the mountain like it grew there, gray stone walls the color of the rock beneath them, towers that catch the light and throw it back cold. The town huddles around the base of it the way towns do around power, close enough to feel safe, far enough to remember their place.

My ring rests comfortable on my finger, warm still. It has changes somehow since that strange moment in the forest. I can see writing along the inside of the band, but cannot decipher it, Alcor claims it's Elvish. The wagon rolls through the main gate and I walk alongside it, one hand absently turning the ring on my finger the way I have found myself doing since the temple. The guards at the gate watch us pass with the particular boredom of men who have stood in the same spot too long. One of them glances at the ring and then away. Nothing worth noting. Just a caravan. Tired travelers come to trade and leave without a second thought.

Towser steals a glance at me as we ride along the cobbled road past the gate, I can feel his smirk digging into the back of my head. "Stop fiddling with your trinket, you'll wear a groove into your finger."

I don't answer him. He loses interest quickly when I don't bite.

The streets here are cut narrow, tight and dug right between buildings of the same dark stones the castle is built on. If there is any wood here, it's sparsely used, this is a stone city. The people passing move the same way, heads down, eyes low, purposeful. They aren't sad, they are simply directed. This is not a city that lingers.

You hear things on the road, rumors, strange tales, Tarnkeep was no exception to the traveler's gossip. The ruling family were princes of Kheagon once, they ruled from Highstride, the capital of the region. Tarnkeep had been their ancestral home through that period, but the Tarn family had been overthrown and ousted by the Ferrans. They say the Tarn's carry old blood, magic, sorcerous. By all accounts it's likely true, wouldn't be surprised some isolated mountain folk had some fun with a spirit or two. A merchant in the last town lowered his voice when he mentioned them, which told me more than his words did.

Alcor finds us lodgings near the merchant quarter, two rooms above a chandler's shop that smells of tallow and faintly of something sweeter underneath it. Lukas takes the larger room without waiting. He always does when his back is bad. I have never called him on it and I won't start now. I leave them to it and set out back into the streets while there is still light enough to navigate by.

Alcor gave me a name to follow, a Half-Elf scholar who has studied celestial and Elven histories. Apparently he trades in secrets and items, both of which I believe reside within my ring. He has a shop somewhere in the merchant quarter. A blue lamp in the window is how you find him, I was told. Not magical, just blue glass, but it stands out on a street of yellow candlelight. Anything to make the masses wonder about your power is good for business.

The ring warms slightly as I walk. It does that sometimes around old things. Around magic. I have started to pay attention to when it happens and when it doesn't. The blue lamp is easy enough to find. I push the door open.

The smell hits first. Old parchment, ink, and something mineral underneath like wet stone. Books line every wall floor to ceiling, stacked in the gaps between, piled on the floor in arrangements that have their own internal logic I cannot begin to read. A gray cat sleeps on a closed grimoire near the door and opens one eye at me without interest.

The scholar is behind the counter, Gods he looks old. He must have lived through the Uncrowned Rebellion, an elf looking like he did, wrinkles and withered hair, means he has been alive many hundreds of years, perhaps an Age... He doesn't look up from his table, but his cat purrs in response to my entry. I unclip my sheath and leave my blade by the door, a sign of trust and respect in shops and other establishments.

I stand before the desk at a respectful distance, unable to see what he is working on. Strangely, I recognize the amulet on his neck, a red bird, the symbol of an academy in the East. "Hello Sage. I am Inayet, my caravan is visiting here, and I heard you were a man who knew things. I found an interesting item I thought you could look at for me, there's elvish writing, an old celestial dialect. If it's not too much trouble."

I slide some coins across the table, gold ones. I counted 10, half a trip's wage. It's a hefty sum, but this is specialized work for an expert. I want this read right, if the ring is a magic artifact, speaking the wrong words could be dangerous.

He speaks slowly and quietly, like he is hiding his words from a nearby onlooker. "I am always happy to investigate any oddities. Of course there is the matter of payment... which you have easily satisfied. I will say this, if I do not tell you something new, I take no payment. Hopefully I can shed light on your curiosity Knight. Now, what have you brought me?"

I remove my glove, and slip the ring off my finger, it is nearly hot now, strange. Perhaps the Sage has awakened something in it. "Not a Knight, Sage. Just a warrior. And what I've brought, is this ring." I place it down on the table, it startled me, the sound it makes on the table is heavy, like it weighed ten pounds.

He slips a pair of gloves on his own hands, they are ornate, lined with gold filaments. Artificing gloves, designed for identifying magical items, and protecting yourself from curses. "Look there, along the inside of the band, that writing. That's what I'm here for, I can't read the words."

He twists the ring on every possible angle, moving his glasses as close to it as he can. "I see no writing Sir."

My face twists in confusion, "You need better glasses friend. Look here, it's clear as day, blue lettering on the inside."

He looks more confused than I do. "I cannot see it, but you can... fascinating." His face changes at that moment, like this revelation has made my transaction worth caring about. He studies me more than he studies the ring now.

He hands me back my ring, and I return it to my finger. "You have tomes on language? If I can see it, I can learn the words."

He laughs at that. "Are you a learned warrior then, Inteya? The Elvish tongue is not easy even for the mages to learn, especially a celestial dialect... perhaps that is not such a wise idea."

"My name is Inayet. Sell me the tome Sage, and let me worry about my ability to learn it." I feign bravado, something a real Knight would do. I probably can't learn the words, but it is better to try.

"No, no, warrior Inayet. Make no mistake. I do not hesitate because you are unable. I hesitate because you do not know what you are entering with this artifact." He says this, but he contradicts his words with actions, standing and gathering a book from his many shelves. He places it on the table, and slides it over to me. I pick it up, the cover is washed leather, this is a very old book. As I inspect my purchase, he gathers the coins I put on the table, every last one.

"This is an expensive book then hm?" I ask jokingly.

He chuckles out a, "Very..."

I turn to leave, gathering my weapon, but he calls out before I am out the door. "Warrior! This is not a warning, but advice. Special artifacts do not tend to stay with regular men for long..."

I conceal my worry by turning away, but I know he is right. I am a regular man.

"Heed those words! If you are without purpose the ring will make one, it can be a tool or a burden to you!"

I close the door gently behind me, and stand taking a deep breath beneath the blue lantern. I study the ring on my finger, wondering if I am strong enough to carry a powerful magical item like this seems to be, or if it will consume me like some classic tragedy.

---

I am resolved to the fact that I will never learn Elvish. The tome lies open, same as my mouth. No doubt if there were flies in my room they would have flown in and made home there. I must have been staring at the bloody thing for hours. It's nearly midnight, and I cannot understand the letters. They are written in magic ink, a quill that defies logic and makes a man mad.

The only comfort is the ring, warm on my hand, not hot and desperate, it is calm, almost like I can feel its patience. What is it waiting for, what does it want from me?

I don't know yet, but I am frustrated, and in frustration I snatch the cover of the book, ready to slam it shut and toss it across the room. My hand finds the tome, but I freeze my hand before I can do something I regret, and take in a deep breath.

A knock on the door interrupts my moment of calming, and a voice. "Inayet, you awake? I see a light under the door, you shouldn't leave candles lit while you sleep." Lukas.

"Come in Petlia, you're a godsend. You just saved the life of this poor tome." I joke as he enters. He is carrying a wine bottle, and wearing his plain clothes. I love that green shirt of his, its heavy and warm, I borrowed it one cold night in the winter years ago. Been envying it ever since, he says its woven from Ironwood threads, but I know you can't make shirts out of timber.

He looks confusedly at the book in my hand, and I turn it over to him, showing the cover. "Ah, learning Elvish? I speak a bit myself. Did you know my ancestor attended the second Champion's Convergence? He met every Divine Champion in the world. It's a great honor."

"I didn't know that Petlia, very interesting. You must be proud." I say plainly, setting the tome on my nightstand. i had heard that a hundred times, but reminding him would only make me feel bad.

His hands idle for a moment before he beckons me out of my room. "Come on, got something to show you." Before I can protest, he is out the door and I hear the creaking of the stairs.

I throw on my boots and cloak, covering myself as best I can. Mountain town will be chilly at night. I make my way to the front and exit the building, bundling my pelt around me, seeing my breath in front of me. It's biting cold, even for the season. I catch sight of Petlia waving at me, he's shambling up some steps, grunting in effort from exerting his tired knees. I have no idea where he's going, but I'd rather not leave him alone where ever he is headed, so I follow. 

I catch up with him quickly, we are ascending stone steps carved into the hills beside the place we have taken rooms in. It takes him a while, but we reach the end. A small balcony carved in the side of the hills. It's beautiful, mountain above, dug right out of the hill, small pillars of stone split the overlook like windows. 

Lukas stops, grunting as he falls into a chair, and I beside him. We sit for a moment, taking in the sight. Everything north of Tarnkeep is visible, the night is clear and bright under the moons. I crane my neck to count. 

"Three moons tonight." I say, "There's misty little Nysria, Thalun the blue, and the yellow one, Arrak."

He sighs calmly, more like releasing a deep breath filled with stress than anything else. "A barkeep told me about this, said it was a nice place to finish a bottle of wine."

I laugh, snatching the bottle from his hands, "Then let's listen to the local!" I take a swig of the wine, nothing but air. "You vagrant! You drank this whole thing?"

He nods lazily, he's not so drunk, but not so sober either.

"I did. Apologies. I tried to save some for you, but I think I may have forgotten you can drink now." He rubs his temple gently. "You turned 18 so recently..."

I hadn't been 18 in many years. Many years... he's going worse than I had thought. Every healer I've met with said it's best to humor him, and I had met with many.

"That's alright Lukas. I don't drink much anyway." I nudge his shoulder, and feel him shaking, he didn't bring a cloak.

"You're cold." I say matter-of-factly, opening the arm of my cloak to him, offering to share its warmth. "Come in here oh Knight of the Horned Helm, and warm your old heart."

He laughs and leans in, our chairs bumping one another. We share warmth, like many times before. Petlia is a good man, and sitting her bundled together reminds me of many times I was younger when he shielded me from the elements and enemies alike.

"I've been meaning, to give you this. It's a gift, but it's also a burden you see. If you say no I'll understand." He says, producing his helmet from beneath his seat. The antlers are gone, and instead on its sides are Sparrows, painted across its cheeks. "I had it made today, I hope you like it boy."

He holds it out to me, but I push it back into his chest. "Even if I could accept such a generous gift, your fighting days aren't over yet. We have many miles left to walk together, and not to mention a gift with a job attached. What's this burden I'm about to accept?"

He laughs, and with a burst of strength I am surprised he can still muster, he shoves the helmet onto me. "When I die, bring it home for me Sparrow. The Ironwood, what remains of my family's forest. Bring it there, leave it somewhere, marked, my grave doesn't matter. Where you leave my arms does."

He meets my eyes, and I see a fire in him like I haven't seen in years. "You're a good sword, you're a good man, and I did everything I could for you. I think you're become a fine warrior Inayet, and I would be honored if you would do this for me. I don't... have any other sons to ask."

I can see the water in the corner of his eyes. "Your wine is talking Old Man." I say half joking, but I wrap my arm around his shoulder and pull him into a hug. "Of course I will bring your helmet home"

"I worry about you boy. You've got all the tools for greatness, better than greatness, kindness. You've always been capable of it, but you're drifting. I worry that you may get stuck, nothing wrong with being a caravan guard, only wrong with never trying to be more. Maybe when i pass, you'll be free to find yourself." I can feel the hitching in his breath as he holds back tears.

"I'll be more likely to find my grand destiny with you around to smack me upside the head when it walks by Lukas. Don't go dying on me anytime soon."

He doesn't answer, we just sit in silence, warm beneath my cloak together. The moons are so still, the sounds of crickets in the night, laughter as people enter the taverns below for drinks and merrymaking. The cool air on my skin is like drinking a cool glass of water on a hot day. I don't think I realized how long I've been at this job. I took a few months here and there off, but going on 10 years working nearly constantly has not been kind. 

Lukas has mentioned his worries about me before, he's the closest thing I have to a father and I appreciate his concern. I just don't feel it yet. I found something I enjoy, why break that up? There had been chances, hunts for a great spirit to make my fortune as a hunter, swearing oaths to Princes and become a knight (as if I would ever do that), but none had ever lit the fire in my like I had seen in Petlia. I was holding out for that, the purpose truly great people find within.

I lift my right hand and slowly spin the ring around my finger, feeling its warmth very clearly now. Maybe this ring will be the key, something great is connected to it, and I can become great with that. But what is the purpose of greatness? Strength is my tool for work, and for doing right, being great is more opportunity for good, and bad. I had seen men rise, high into the heights of power, a magic sword, a bargain with a Fey, it ends the same.

I adjust my shoulder, illicit a snort from Petlia, he's fallen asleep. I won't wake him, best he gets the rest, and I don't mind. I watch the stars, and by some small miracle, I see one falling. A shooting star, on a night like this. I raise my hand to point and show Lukas, but remember he is asleep, and let him rest. That was just for me to enjoy, and I make a silent wish. A wish for purpose, for meaning, and to help someone who really needs it the way Lukas Petlia had helped me.

I see the star blink for a brief second, did it hear my wish? A small gasp escapes my mouth as I feel the ring on my hand, warmer than ever, almost painful, and then in the next second it is gone.

"Just what are you?" I ponder to the ring, my answer is strange. I head a faint nicker, almost like one of the horses. My head spins, but the sound vanishes in an instant, and I am left to wonder if it happened at all.
