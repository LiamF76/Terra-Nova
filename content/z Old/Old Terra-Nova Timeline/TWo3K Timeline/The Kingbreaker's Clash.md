The Kingbreaker’s Clash

The day Adrithyr and Calix clashed was a bloody and sad exchange. To see brothers quarrel is common, but to see them draw swords with the intent to kill is another thing entirely. Calix had declared himself independent, and named himself king of his new Kingdom “Calixos”, some say the name is daft (I would tend to agree) but it was a powerful declaration. He had secretly dealt with Lord Torin Wolfsbane of Dort Duregar, and had amassed a decent army after Adrithyr’s many losses to his rebel son Ivar. Calix was not the strongest general or warrior, but his lands were rich from the Mithral and Silver mines, and he was a master of politics and statecraft.

Using this mastery he created a very powerful position from a seemingly feeble one. After his staunch declaration of independence from Estor, Calix and his family fled the capital of Terraven. Calix along with his wife and trusted arcane advisor Miranda Greyscale, traveled through the Mythwood forest toward the valley of Calixos, hoping to reach safe haven in Calix’s new capital city of Mythria. 

Adrithyr has been slipping in his rule for years, slowly but steadily, and this brought his anger to a head. He ordered his strongest legions, 2 troops of 500 men each to pursue and apprehend Calix, dead or alive. “Estor, my kingdom, a breeding ground for bastards and traitors,” he said. The legions deployed quickly, and as mounter knights led by two of Adrithyr’s greatest generals, Sirs Kael and Tharlic. They quickly caught the escaping Lord Calix and his companions. They reached a hilltop just above the entrance to the valley, where they spotted the small group of traitors. Calix quickened the carriage's pace, but as the 1000 mounted men charged down towards them, it was obvious he was doomed.

Kael, known for his ferocity and lack of restraint, was the first down the hill, and the first to fall. Arrows riddled his body as a massive cohort of Mythrian knights charged from the forest surrounding the hill, and began cleaving through the ranks of the unsuspecting Royal army. Calix had laid a perfect trap, knowing Adrithyr could not resist pursuing him due to his ensuing rage. He had planted himself as bait to lure the cavalry legion into the close quarters valley and give himself disadvantage despite having half of Kael and Tharlic’s soldiers. 

Calix stood atop his carriage laughing in triumph, having won a battle that Adrithyr had started, without even drawing his sword.

In the words of Sir Stannis Atwood, Calix’s greatest knight and leading general, “That stupid fuck Kael. He ran straight into a cloud of Mithril arrows. Brave dumb bastard. After he fell, the battle was all but won. He carried all their fire, their hearts. I broke their hearts, and then their bodies.”

Atwood ended the melee in single combat with Tharlic, and in a studding show of skill the two warriors clashed. Atwood called the battle a  “fair contest, but Tharlic was not the duelist I am, he was dead the moment he entered my lands.” The two swordsmen had fought many times in tournaments, but Tharlic was just a tournament knight, and he was young. Stannis was battle hardened, having waged war with the likes of legendary warriors like Igan and Tyran during The War of Miracles. Atwood took up his shield, painted with a great silver tree (A Mythwood tree), and batted away Tharlic’s hammer as he swung wildly, the hammer dented the shield, which was a great show of strength, but the vibration shuddered Tharlic’s nerves, and took his balance. Atwood swiftly dropped, sweeping his leg under Tharlic and dropping him on his ass in the mud. Tharlic looked up in horror for a moment, uttered a single plea, and Atwood drove his sword through the young knight's throat.

Although Calix is credited with the strategy, it was clearly Atwood’s battle plan, Calix assembled the men, and Atwood positioned them. Stannis was respected before this battle, but this threw his name into the hat of the great knights of Escarth, and earned him the title of “Kingbreaker”. They say this battle was the final straw, the deciding factor that led Adrithyr into true madness, thanks to Stannis Atwood.
