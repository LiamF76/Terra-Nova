## The Checkpoint

Figures move atop the wall — soldiers in leather and steel, their crossbows resting lazily on the battlements as they watch the road. A wooden gate yawns open just wide enough to allow carts and travelers through one at a time. You hear faint shouts, the clink of chains, and the whinny of horses up ahead. 

The wooden gates loom ahead, framed by a pair of watchtowers where crossbowmen track your approach with lazy, watchful eyes. A dozen soldiers patrol the area, checking carts and asking travelers questions, while a few merchants grumble about delays.

Standing just inside the gate is a striking figure — a Tiefling woman with crimson skin like banked coals, her horns polished and curving back over her braided black hair. She leans casually against a massive warhammer planted in the dirt, its shaft as thick as a man’s wrist and its steel head gleaming with faint runes. Her golden eyes study the road with predatory calm.

As you near the checkpoint, she pushes off the warhammer, strides forward with purpose, and stops squarely in your path. Her armor, blackened steel etched with a symbol you don't recognize, rattles slightly as she folds her arms.

The symbol is of a maze on fire. This is the Symbol of Lazaryth, the Holy City.

(She trails off, eyeing Thalia as if she remembers something, an order or something to look out for that is just on the tip of her tongue.) There was an order out to watch for young girls traveling with a warforged. Admirals said the academy had a claim to her, might that be you little one?
- **If the search goes well:** You check out. No blood on your hands I can see. I’ll let you through — but don’t make me regret it. Traxgate’s not as forgiving as I am. As you break past the checkpoint’s heavy gates, the path bends gently southward, flanked by spurts of forest—tall oaks and whispering aspens clustering in pockets like old watchful spirits. Birds flit overhead in bursts of color, their songs trailing off as your footsteps crunch over the packed dirt road.

Somewhere to your left, you catch a glimpse of a ruined stone arch half-swallowed by ivy, the last remnant of an old border marker or perhaps a shrine now forgotten. A stream gurgles faintly beyond the tree line, its waters flashing silver in the sunlight.

The air grows warmer here—still holding a bite of autumn, but laced with a faint sweetness from the turning leaves. The road’s solitude invites conversation, or perhaps quiet reflection as the miles begin to stretch behind you.

## Redwick's Grave

As the road levels out, you see it: a modest town nestled in the shallow embrace of a wide valley. Redwick’s wooden palisades rise sturdily from the earth, ivy creeping up the posts like nature reclaiming its stake. Beyond the gates, you catch glimpses of red-tiled roofs and thin columns of chimney smoke curling lazily into the pale autumn sky.

The sign above the entrance reads simply **"Redwick"**, but someone—long ago or perhaps more recently—has scrawled an epithet beneath in blackened letters: **"Redwick’s Grave."** Whether it’s a warning, a memorial, or a crude joke is hard to say.

The smell of bread and roasting meats wafts from within, carried on the breeze alongside the faint sounds of hammering and laughter. Farmers guide carts of apples and late-season gourds through the gates, while a pair of guards in simple boiled leather eye you with mild curiosity. A tinkling bell above the sign swings gently as you ride past. Redwick is no grand city, but it feels alive, like a heartbeat amid the quiet plains and scattered woods you’ve crossed.

A sign points down the dirt streets, indicating the way to the tavern, "**The Rusty Lantern**". Additionally you hear the sounds of commotion from the other direction, towards the local garrison. You can hear shouting, lots of it, some kind of argument or protest.

#### Tavern

The door to the Rusty Lantern creaks loudly as you push it open, letting in a gust of cool autumn air that stirs the smoky haze inside. The scent of roasted meat, stale ale, and melted tallow hits you first, mingling with the faint tang of iron from the old lanterns that hang on soot-stained beams overhead.
The tavern is dim but cozy, lit by wagon-wheel chandeliers strung with fat, dripping candles. Their wax has pooled onto the wooden tables below, where tired farmers and fur-clad trappers huddle over mugs, their conversations low and tense.
At the far corner, a cluster of townsfolk argue in heated whispers, their voices rising above the lute music drifting from a lone bard by the hearth.
"It’s the ruined temple, I tell you. That’s where it started," hisses an older man, his knuckles white around his mug. And the Traxgate guards? Bah—they’ll sit on their hands till we’re all worm-food.
A pair of city guards in tarnished armor sit at the bar, looking uncomfortable as they sip from chipped cups and try to ignore the glares sent their way. One of them mutters something under his breath about "Gravebinder’s hand" and spits on the floor.
Behind the bar, a stocky man with a greying beard and a lantern tattooed on his forearm gives you a wary once-over. He wipes his hands on a rag and leans forward.
"You look like folk who can handle trouble. If you’re staying, take a seat and keep your ears open. This lot’s ready to boil over."

#### Garrison

The shouting grows louder as you turn down a narrow cobblestone street lined with weathered timber houses and vegetable carts left half-unattended. A crowd has gathered at the edge of Redwick’s central square, their voices sharp and angry.
At the heart of the commotion stands a small orc girl, barely four feet tall, her tangled braids of starry hair bound in scraps of cloth and feathers. Though young, her voice carries the strength of a storm as she waves a wooden staff capped with an antler shed.
"People are dying out there! Graves splitting open! You wait for Traxgate’s permission, and my neighbors rot in their beds!"
Around her, a knot of townsfolk nod and murmur their agreement, some clutching pitchforks or hoes, though most look too frightened to act.
Opposite them stand two city guards, their armor bearing the green-and-bronze crest of Traxgate. One, a weary-looking sergeant with a wiry mustache, holds up his gauntleted hands.
They do not answer, but they appear unfeeling towards her plight, content to stand and wait for them to leave.

**The Guards:** Traxian soldiers, not knights, and very happy to stay in town and not deal with the undead issue at the temple. They are holding fast to the fact that they do not act without orders.

**Cerwan and the mob:** Cerwan is a young druid and was trained by Ryfon at the Oakenheart. She has stopped the undead from entering town, but a new undead mage has arrived, she believes it is Gravebinder, and she can't keep him at bay herself. Wants the adventurers help, in exchange she will give them information on the [[Wings of Pyrahn]] (Artifact of Illuman).

## Graveyard Encounter

The journey out of Redwick takes you down a winding dirt path, flanked by fields left fallow for the season. The autumn wind bites harder here, rustling through brittle stalks and sending leaves skittering across the road like fleeing insects. Cerwan walks ahead of you, her small frame cloaked in furs dyed a deep green. Though young, her pace is steady and purposeful, a staff of pale oak clutched tightly in her hand.

As the path slopes upward, the trees thin to reveal a black iron gate twisted by age. Beyond it sprawls the graveyard—a sea of crooked headstones and leaning monuments, shrouded in a faint silver mist. Ancient oaks stand sentry within, their skeletal branches tangled like grasping fingers. Somewhere deeper, past the graves, you can just make out the silhouette of the ruined temple’s spire, half-collapsed against the horizon.

The air here is... wrong. Heavy. Each breath carries a faint sweetness like rotting flowers, and an almost imperceptible chill prickles at your skin.

Cerwan stops abruptly at the gate, her boots scraping against the gravel. She turns to you, her tusked mouth drawn into a tight frown.

"This is as far as I go. The spirits here... they don’t take kindly to my kind. I’ll stay back and watch the tree line. If any stragglers escape you, I’ll see them turned to ash." She plants the butt of her staff into the earth, and faint green motes of druidic light swirl briefly at her feet. "But the temple... that’s your mission. May Illuman’s light guide you, or whatever gods you trust."

The wind gusts again, carrying with it the faint sound of rattling—bones? Chains? Or just the trees whispering warnings?

As you continue to the gates of the graveyard, a faint whisper, and a wispy golden form of a man appears, a ghost! He wears holy robes, and holds an amulet in his heart. He bows before you, holds out his amulet to you, and points, indicating into the graveyard.
If they understand that he wants them to find his amulet, he will nod enthusiastically!

## Temple Ruins Encounter

The path winds upward from the crypt, choked with cracked flagstones and half-buried in dead leaves. As you crest the rise, the full scale of the ruined temple comes into view. Once a monument to Illuman’s glory, it now stands as a blackened husk against the gray sky. Its spires are shattered, its marble walls fractured and veined with creeping necrotic energy that pulses faintly like veins beneath skin.

The temple courtyard is littered with debris and desecrated idols—sun motifs gouged out, their golden inlays stripped and melted into crude symbols of darkness. The air smells of old ash and wet earth, heavy with the chill of unnatural stillness.

And then you see them.
- A towering **Death Knight**, clad in scorched black plate, the faint glow of red runes tracing across his armor. His hand rests idly on the hilt of a greatsword as long as a man is tall. A faint, hollow rasp escapes his helm, almost like breathing—or perhaps the rattle of chains.
- Beside him, draped in tattered robes of midnight blue, floats a **Deathlock Mastermind**. Its withered face is half-hidden by a cracked bone mask, and in its hands swirl twin orbs of sickly green flame. Necrotic energy crawls along the ground where it drifts, causing nearby grass to wither and rot.

As you step into the courtyard, both figures turn to face you in perfect unison. The Death Knight’s sword scrapes free of its scabbard with a sound like tearing metal, and the Deathlock raises a skeletal hand, whispering words that send a ripple of dark energy rolling across the courtyard stones. You can all sense that these are powerful foes. Roll initiative!
