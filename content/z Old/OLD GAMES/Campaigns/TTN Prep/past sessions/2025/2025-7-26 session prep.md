## The Road to the Fortress

As you gather your things in the morning after your victory against the Death Knight and servants of [[Gravebinder]], you mount up your horses, and begin away on the road. Before you can reach the edge of the village, you are chased down by a Goblinoid horse, which strangely morphs into Cerwan the Druid!

As she jogs alongside you, she gives thanks for your help protecting your village, and tosses a small pouch holding a piece of amber and a parchment with a poem on it to Emrys. "Good luck finding Pyrahn's treasures!" She shouts as she begins to slow, allowing you all to gallop away. "You're just as brave as Ryfon said you'd be!" Is her final statement of goodbye.

The hours slip by as your horses clop steadily over the frost-bitten dirt road, the sharp chill of the northern winter fading as you push further south. Here, beyond the Great Divide, the air carries the earthy scent of fallen leaves and cold rain instead of snow. The landscape has shifted too—dense evergreens give way to sparse clumps of autumn-bare trees, their copper and russet leaves clinging stubbornly in the crisp breeze. The plains beyond roll gently, painted in muted browns and golds, the last colors of the dying season.

Far in the distance, on the far edge of the lowlands, rises the imposing silhouette of **Traxgate**. The city’s walls are impossibly high, black stone and iron blending together like jagged teeth biting into the sky. Beyond those walls, slender towers rise like spears, their tips catching the weak sunlight through a shifting red haze.

But it’s not just the city that draws your eyes. Beyond Traxgate lies the vast expanse of the **Sea of Smoke**—a boiling, churning mass of crimson and ash-grey clouds that swirl violently over dark waters. Occasional flashes of sickly green lightning fork across its depths, followed by low, distant rumbles that roll across the land like the growl of some slumbering titan. This is the edge of the **Scar**, the gaping wound across the world where reality itself was torn asunder. It is noticeably smaller than at its apex, but it is still terrifying and evil all the same.

The air feels heavier here, thick with the faint scent of soot and something faintly metallic. Your horses shift uneasily as if they too sense the wrongness emanating from the distant hellstorm. It is late afternoon, about 5pm.

The road ahead is long still, but with every step you draw nearer to the city that may decide your future.

Anyone have anything to say, coming into view of the city, or perhaps the violence and terror of the Scar, which seems amplified here especially? Ivys you have also noticed, there are no bodies hanging in the trees, no ropes, no sign of Hannibal's gruesome order.

## The Forgotten Armory

The streets of Traxgate grow quieter as Emrys slips into the Dust District, where the city’s past crumbles into neglect. The Forgotten Armory looms ahead—a squat, fortress-like structure of blackened stone and rusted iron grates, its once-mighty gates now sagging on broken hinges. A faded banner flaps in the cold wind: the sigil of crossed swords, now half-obscured by the new emblem of Aldred, Lord of Penance—a bleeding gauntlet clutching a broken chain.

The Entrance:  
A hooded acolyte stands at the door, face hidden in shadow. Without a word, they push open the creaking iron door, revealing a narrow stairwell descending into flickering torchlight. The air smells of cold metal, incense, and old blood.

Inside the Temple:  
The vast chamber of the old armory has been transformed. Weapon racks now hold votive candles, their wax dripping like molten silver over rusted swords. Tattered battle standards hang from the ceiling like funeral shrouds. At the far end, where a forge once roared, now stands a makeshift altar—a slab of pitted steel, upon which lies a single, scarred longsword, its blade notched with a hundred sins.

The Congregation:  
A handful of broken warriors kneel in silence, their heads bowed. Some wear the colors of disgraced knightly orders, others the rags of deserters. Their hands are bound in strips of bloodstained cloth—the mark of those seeking Aldred’s absolution.

Brother Tarot:  
A monk in a tattered surcoat sits near the altar, his milky eyes unseeing as his fingers trace the edges of a well-worn tarot deck. He does not look up as Emrys enters, but his lips twitch in recognition.

"You are expected," he murmurs. "The Lord of Penance does not summon lightly."

He turns to face you Emrys, and you take in his true face. His skin is black, almost shining, with horns of gold and pale yellow eyes. He is an exotic and confusing beauty.

As you approach he beckons calmly, inviting you to stand beside him at the altar. "They call me the keeper of the scales, I merely put things on them to be balanced. You have been feeling unbalanced Mr. Marcell, have you not?"

"How can this priest be of service to you then child?"

"How do you feel about yourself? When you think of who you are, what you have done, what do you think?"'

He draws a card from the deck, The High Priestess, but reversed. "This card represents repressed feelings, lost inner voice, lack of acceptance, waiting for something to be forgiven. My only question is why?"

"I will say this to you Mr. Marcell. I know quite a bit about you, perhaps more than any mortal. I am quite angry with you. What you have done many consider unforgivable. What you have done... denying yourself the pleasures of life, refusing forgiveness for your transgression against my Lord, truly sinful. You have made mistakes, and you allow them to burrow into your mind, never leaving your immediate thoughts, yet you forget and minimize the good, all the wonderful good that you do. This will go on no longer. This is your penance Mr. Marcell. While you are here in Traxgate, and for the rest of your adventure, I demand you give yourself more credit for your deeds, and enjoy yourself. You ae not evil, and in the eyes of the Black Sun, you have long been forgiven. It is time you saw that in your eyes too."

"Come, let us go home. Dinner will be waiting at my home, and hopefully Promise has not drank all my rum and wine before your friends had a chance at the cellar, I have heard your allies enjoy a good vintage."
