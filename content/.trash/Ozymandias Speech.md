By the gods how I hated Caelus. I hated the head, the dust, the thick sweat of it. Even before I knew there were other towns and cities I cursed Shao Daraan for making it so unbearable. There's a reason religion prospers in wastelands... there's nothing else to do but ponder the misery.
I used to sit in the doorway of my father's house, squatting, watching the waves of heat along the empty expanse. I'd wait for scorpions to scuttle out of the glare. I would pluck them, hold them up, watching them wriggle. I plucked their libs off one by one. Sometimes I got stung... sometimes I didn't. It was a kind of game, though, not a very good one.
Once when brother was out hunting, being a brave warrior for the village, I was stung so badly I suffered a fever for 3 weeks. I lay on the stone floor writing, suffering boiling internal heat, and then began the visions. I might have died, I didn't care much either way. 
Once I had recovered I was back on the doorstep, waiting for the scorpions to scamper into my grasp. Even since then I've played the exact same game. Find the danger, get close to it, hold it in the palm of your hand, see how long you can stand it until it bites you, or you bite it. 
The marks on my flesh...
My parents were exasperated with me. They wanted me to learn a trade, become a warrior like Lazarus, study the weave and magics, something useful to do. I didn't want any of that. I wanted to be rich without trying. I wanted to have slaves and concubines, and play my scorpion game with people. For a long time it wasn't clear how I would be able to achieve that, but fate had a way of leading me into opportunity.
Being the observant sort I had noticed that the Church of Stories had become a sort of organization I could thrive in. Many think it to be the precursor to your holy and honest Hyperion Church. It was not so. The priests in the settlements beyond the civilized cities had earned a sort of... reputation.
Their prayers were violently powerful, but they stopped saying Shao... they simply said Daraan. And when i woke from my visions, my prayers extended out to Shao and I heard nothing. Unlike my brother, all gods answered to him. I prayed to Daraan, nameless Daraan, the earth itself the world as it was and would be... and something answered. Something that promised me everything I had ever wanted, my slaves, my power, my scorpion game. All I had to do was kill my brother. 
I did. Simple really, he forsook his pact with Vulcan, and I drove my knife through his back that very evening, but he didn't die. Shao cursed the great hero who wanted to rest with eternal life, and he rose, forgave me, and left. 
It ENRAGED me. To think my power was so miniscule that it was cast aside and forgiven as if I was a fly who had stung him, my brother not seeing me as enough of a threat to do anything to other than FORGIVE!  But then I met the follower, the first follower of the nameless Daraan. The God in Glass. He told me that if I wanted to fulfill my patron's wish, I would need the same curse. I begged him for it, give me life and I will chase Lazarus to the ends of the earth and beyond. The God in Glass laughed, and said that I would suffer for my desire, endlessly. He said that all I needed to stay alive was his touch, and the name of our shared lord.
I took his hands on bended knee, and said, tell me his name.
He smiled and held my hand tightly saying quietly, almost a whisper, "Khal Daraan."
Of course, no make knows the intentions of the ever elusive God in Glass, and when I realized what was happening it was too late. He trapped me here, in this great prison, Thalun, your blue moon. 
But now I am free, thanks to you. Free to play my scorpion game, and free to liberate my master. Goodbye little flies.
