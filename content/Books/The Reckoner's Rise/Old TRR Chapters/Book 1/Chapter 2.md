
Ivar tumbled into his bed. His body was exhausted, and he was thankful for the day off tomorrow. A holiday for the village, the Summer Solstice had been long anticipated, and Ivar loved the celebrations. The townsfolk would light a grand bonfire and toss loaves into it, if they blackened instantly, it was an omen for war and famine, but if they slowly browned, the year would be prosperous. 

Ivar placed his books on his desk, they took up almost the entire thing, but he was grateful for the space he had. There is only one room for permanent housing in the Leaky Log, and Chian lived there year round. She had been kind enough to let him stay in one of the storage closets upstairs. A small room beneath the stairs to the third floor, it was cramped, but it was cozy, and he loved it. It was the only space that was truly his. His books piled on his little desk, the warm fur blankets and mattress on his bed (which took up more than half of the room), it was home.

As he lay in bed he looked up at the wooden ceiling, continuing to daydream, but his fantasy was interrupted by the strange vision he had seen when he touched the stranger's hand. What was that, a vision of some kind induced by this strange man? Was he a mage, if so what was he doing in Rookswood, perhaps he was visiting the Green Wizard, or maybe the Lord in Felldoor? Ivar knew he could spin endless questions in his head, but the only way to get a real answer was to ask Chian. Ivar leapt up and nervously stepped down the stairs, peering around the banister to peek at Chian behind the bar. Her mood mattered more than anything, if a local had been rowdy enough to throw a drink, or worse, she would cuss at him and send the boy hurrying up the stairs again.

She seemed content enough, her big orcish barely fit behind that tiny bar, but she made do. She was using that same dirty old rag to scrub out a tankard, before she popped another beer and slid it down toe counter to another customer who didn't care if the cup had been cleaned at all. She chuckled at something Wilton said, which was good. If the sour old elf can make a joke good enough for Chian to laugh at, she must be overflowing with joy.

Ivar smiled and continued down the stairs from his watching point. It wasn't as if he had reason to be really afraid of her, he just preferred not be be shouted away from the bar during busy times. He sauntered up to the bar, wanting to appear casual and uninterested, which was his favorite tactic to squeeze information out of his friend Chian. He slid up tot he bar slyly, avoiding her eyeline as she placed a dew more full mugs on the bar for some thirsty patrons. He hopped up into one of the tall bar stools and began to kick his legs, happily putting on a face without worry, despite how much his vision had concerned him. Despite that, his acting was sensational, he appeared jolly, unfocused, not a care in the world

"And what do you want Ivar, something has you spooked tonight. Its written on you face plain as day." Chian chuckled from behind the bar.

Damn. Ivar thought to himself, his cover had been blown. "Well not really, saw someone interesting though! He was leaving the Log, big cape, who was he?" Ivar threw his caution to the wind, he had a question, and he thought an answer was not much to ask. Who was that stranger?

Chian shook her head no, "His name is none of your business, and his business is far above any of our heads. Some wizard from a far away land kid, leave it at that. Magic like that just gets you killed, or worse."

Ivar began to protest, but Chian silenced him, and sent him off to his room for the evening. Ivar took to his bed again upset at the wall Chian had thrown in his face. There was more to the Wizard than she was telling him, and he would find out what, on his own if he had to. He spent the evening reading his stories, the chronicles of heroes through the ages, and doodling in his journal. He drew himself from the vision, older, stronger, taller, a beard and suit of armor. 

He envisioned himself as a King, but every drawing took shape, as if his hand move with its own mind. As he marveled at his illustrations (though they were crude), despite thinking himself a king, in not one drawing did he wear a crown. Strange, he thought, he always envisioned kings with a crown, and if he ever found a way to the throne he would be sure to wear one, so all his people would know he was there to protect them.

His hells dug into the dirt, his body screaming at him to step aside. The massive clawed hand was coming down on his forearms, and he was going to be overpowered. He needed to move, sidestep and counter, swift punch to the liver and end this fight, but he refused. If he dodged, he would miss the opportunity to kill the beast, and it would run injured, living to return another day. The beast had to die today, this was not up for negotiation. His body screamed in pain as he allowed the claws to lash across his forearm. As the cuts ripped open along his arm, the Drake's claws searing his skin as they touched, he grabbed hold, and gripped the beast with all his might.

Ten years of hard labor, logging, and dragging ores from the mines had built him into a man of stature, and his strength was astounding, though not enough to hold the Drake for long. Its scaled body was suspended, standing on two legs over Ivar as he held its arms, blood pouring down his arms from the cuts, and down his face from the lash on his brow. "ANY DAY NOW BAJURN!" He screamed, the signal for the old Dragonborn to unleash the counter.

As he screamed, a torrent of icy wind erupted from the tree line beside Ivar, Bajurn stood breathing icy death onto the Drake, causing it to bleed and collapse, a freezing mess of death and ice. Ivar collapsed onto his knees, his arms pouring blood onto the road around him. Bajurn ran to the young man, helping him to his feet and tossing his arm over one shoulder, acting as a crutch. "Let's get you to the temple, Davis will patch you up, then we have to get back out there. Damned goblins have burned half the town by now."

Ivar muttered, his voice breaking from the pain as his eyes began to shut, he had lost too much blood. "Don't speak boy. You're gonna sleep for a while. It will only feel like a moment, but when you wake up, grab the nearest blade, and get back in the fight. I'll be waiting for you. You bit off more than you could chew with that Drake, trust your instincts next time."

Ivar nodded with what remained of his energy, seeing the burning haze of Rookswood in front of him as he fell unconscious in Bajurn's arms. 

The goblins had grown more and more bold in the past few years, but this was beyond reasonable. They had Drakes, real Drakes, four legged lizards that could spit fire, where in the hells did goblins in the Western Greenpeaks get drakes? There hadn't been a dragon in Escarth for hundreds of years, drakes yes, but never somewhere so out of the way. The attack had begun days ago, before the people even knew it, the goblins had entered through runoff rivers, following some piping that run beneath the village, old infrastructure from the city ruins Rookswood had been built upon years ago. They had erupted from the earth and woods at once, a coordinated attack, killing and looting within seconds of their presence being noted by the townsfolk.

Ivar had been into the fray from the moment he knew of the attack. After these years of training alongside Bajurn, he was almost excited for a real fight, but that quickly faded. Baj had moved into Rookswood full time as the years wen ton, desiring to settle with the modest fortune he had amassed adventuring for the Firefly. He had taken Ivar on as his apprentice, teaching him everything he could about the world, adventure, and war, and Ivar had taken to it like a natural.

He learned the sword, how to wield it and how to keep it sheathed, because its always better for an opponent to know they will lose, and conceded, than having to prove it with blood. That was according to Bajurn, which Ivar was dubious of, though that was more so due to the fact that he had no idea what it was like to kill.

Ivar felt his chest lurch as he launched himself upwards, a pair of warm hands on his chest. He leapt upwards, his body filling with adrenaline, remembering that Bajurn was out there alone, without him. His head shot around in a panic as a voice called out through the ringing of his head as his mind struggled to keep up with his body's pace. "IVAR LISTEN TO ME! IVAR! Sit down for a dammed second and wake up before you go getting your arms ripped open again."

Ivar could hear Davis, but wasn't totally registering what the man was saying. He looked tired, bags under his eyes, his robes tattered and covered in ash, no doubt from dragging bodies into Illuman's temple. His holy symbol hung from his neck, a heavy cord holding up the gold insignia of a sun on his neck. Ivar finally came to fully, and dove to one knee beside Davis, placing his hand on his friend's shoulder. "Thank you for healing me Davis. Take care of the others, I'll be back, gotta go get Bajurn and put out some fires. Any weapons around?"

Davis looked around for a moment before reaching down to a nearby body, "Old Gilmer had a dagger, crotchety old fool should never have brought a weapon into Illuman's house in the first place." Davis drew the crude hunting knife from the unconscious body's belt, handing it to Ivar, "Walk in the Light brother, and come back alive."

Ivar nodded and stood, holding the knife in his left hand, he burst through the door of the temple, entering the firestorm once again. As he crossed the threshold he offered a short prayer to Illuman. Asking for the the Sun God's light and protection, to give him the strength to protect his village, his family. He felt no response, but that's how the gods were, no clear answers for the regular people. He trudged forward, scanning the town, seeing bodies lain across the road, and green skinned raiders pillaging his home. If Bajurn was anywhere, he would be at the tavern, and Ivar had to get to him. 

He started along the east road, following what remained of the path amongst the hoof prints and debris. As he did he came into contact with a band of goblins, three of them standing should to shoulder, engorging themselves on a dead pony. Ivar didn't hesitate, but he also didn't plan. He charged forward, driving his dagger into the back of one skull, it shrieked for a second before collapsing, dead. Ivar turned to face the other two, the tiny creatures looking up at him in surprise as he interrupted their mean, but he was no longer holding his knife. The dagger had been sunk so deep in the goblins skull that he lost his grip, and now stood unarmed beside two goblins carrying violent blood covered hatchets.

The goblins screeched, the sound sharp and vicious, and lunged at him without thought. Ivar threw himself sideways, the first hatchet singing through the air where his head had been. He landed hard on his shoulder, mud and blood smearing across his torn cloak, but he rolled with the fall, grabbing a broken spear shaft from the ground as he came up.

The second goblin rushed him, mouth wide in a guttural howl. Ivar caught the swing on the shaft of the spear, wood splintering against the force, and drove his boot into the goblin’s knee. Bone cracked and the creature toppled with a yowl, clutching its shattered leg.

The last one came at him, swinging wildly. Ivar ducked low, the hatchet grazing his hair, and thrust the broken spear haft into the goblin’s gut. The goblin staggered back, shrieking, but Ivar ripped the bloodied hatchet from its hand and finished the job with a brutal chop across its throat. He wouldn't back down, he would not surrender, and no matter how tired he got, he would fight until he dropped and then some.

Panting, slick with blood that was not his own, Ivar yanked his dagger free from the first goblin's skull. He did not pause to catch his breath. He wiped the blade on his sleeve and pressed on, limping slightly, moving through the broken ruins of Rookswood.

The east road narrowed into what had once been a pleasant street lined with oaks, but now it was blackened and broken, the trees stripped bare like skeletons reaching to a gray sky. Smoke drifted in heavy sheets across the road, the air thick with the copper stench of blood and the sour bite of burning wood.

He spotted the crooked sign of the Leaky Log Tavern swinging wildly in the wind. The front wall had caved in, the door hanging from one hinge, but the place still stood. That was enough.

Ivar staggered inside, calling out through cracked lips.  
"Bajurn," he rasped. "Bajurn, are you here?"

Silence answered him at first, heavy and hollow. Then he heard the shuffle of boots from deeper within the wreckage. Bajurn dragged himself from a hiding place, his body covered in lacerations and wounds from fighting, an entire Drake tooth stuck out from his left shoulder. His white scales were red and blue with blood and bruising, and he could barely stay upright.

"Bajurn," Ivar breathed, rushing to his friend’s side. Desperate to catch him before he fell.

The dragonborn sagged against him, one hand clamped around the Drake tooth jutting from his shoulder. His other arm hung limp at his side, claws cracked and bloody.

"Still breathing," Bajurn managed through a grimace, his voice a low rumble. "Barely."

Ivar slipped his shoulder under Bajurn’s good arm, hauling him upright with effort. Together they staggered toward the shattered doorway, the ruined street yawning before them.

They had barely made it a dozen steps into the open when the goblins came — five of them, slipping from the rubble like rats from a corpse. Their armor was rusted, their weapons crude, but their eyes gleamed with hunger and spite.

Ivar swallowed the knot in his throat. He pushed Bajurn behind him and lifted his blood-slick dagger and the stolen hatchet. He was getting tired, the energy of Davis' healing was fading.

The first goblin rushed him. Ivar lunged forward too soon, missing his mark, and the goblin slashed his arm open with a jagged blade. Gritting his teeth, Ivar swung back wildly and caught the creature across the face with the side of the hatchet, knocking it down more by luck than aim.

Another darted in. Ivar tried to dodge but stumbled, the goblin’s spear raking across his thigh. He punched at it clumsily, the blow glancing off its shoulder, but Bajurn stepped in behind him, swinging a broken beam like a club. The goblin went sprawling with a shriek.

A third goblin nearly got him — a hatchet whistling past his ear — and Ivar panicked, slashing low with his dagger, catching the goblin’s leg. It screamed and toppled, but not before hacking a deep cut across Ivar’s forearm.

Already breathing hard, blood running down his side, Ivar staggered back toward Bajurn. Every movement was a fight, every step a fresh jolt of pain. He could barely lift the hatchet now, his fingers slick and shaking.

And then the shadow fell over them.

At first, Ivar thought it was smoke. Then he saw the talons gouging the stone and the vast, coiling bulk of the elder drake emerge.

It was enormous, its scales dark as old iron, its wings torn but powerful. Its molten eyes locked on them like a hawk spying a crippled rabbit.

Ivar froze, heart hammering against his ribs.

The drake lunged.

Bajurn tried to drag him away, but Ivar was too slow. The drake’s tail whipped around, catching him full in the chest. The impact was like being hit by a falling tree. He heard something crack inside him — ribs maybe — and then he was airborne, smashed through a rotted wall, landing hard among splinters and ash.

The world tilted and spun. His dagger was gone, his hatchet lost. His whole body screamed in pain. He tried to move and only managed to cough up blood.

Through the haze, he saw Bajurn stumbling toward him, shouting words he could not hear. And beyond him, the elder drake advancing, slow and certain, savoring the hunt.

Ivar clenched his jaw and forced himself onto hands and knees. He would not die lying down.

"Bajurn," he croaked, the word barely more than breath. "Go."

Bajurn hesitated, pain and fury plain on his battered face. But the dragonborn knew the truth. He reached Ivar, dragging him upright with one arm, nearly carrying him as they fled into the smoke.

Behind them, the elder drake loosed a roar that rattled the broken bones of Rookswood.

Ivar did not look back. If he fell, it would not be because he gave up. It would be because there was nothing left.

They limped through the ruined streets, every breath a raw scrape in Ivar’s chest. Bajurn half-dragged him along, his own wounds leaking dark blood down his side. Smoke and dust choked the alleys, turning the world into shifting gray shapes and dying light.

Ahead, through the haze, figures moved — townsfolk, battered and desperate, fleeing in a ragged line. Ivar blinked through the blood in his eyes and saw Davis among them, the old priest clutching a broken staff, his robes torn and bloodstained. Davis caught sight of Ivar and Bajurn and shouted something, voice lost in the chaos, waving them toward the eastern road.

Relief cracked through Ivar's pain, but it lasted only a heartbeat.

The elder drake shrieked above them, its wings beating down dust and ash in great choking clouds. It descended like a nightmare, slamming into the ground between the fleeing villagers and the road.

The earth shook. People screamed and scattered. Davis turned to shield a young boy, raising his staff uselessly as the drake's molten eyes fixed on them.

Ivar tried to run, to throw himself between them, but his legs failed him. He collapsed to one knee, helpless as the drake reared back, jaws gaping wide.

Then Bajurn roared.

The dragonborn hurled himself at the beast without hesitation, swinging the broken beam in both hands. It struck the drake's snout with a cracking sound that barely staggered the creature, but it got its attention.

The elder drake turned, snapping its jaws at Bajurn. He ducked under the strike and drove his shattered weapon into the beast’s eye, the splintered wood sinking deep. The drake howled, thrashing, and Bajurn seized the moment, shouting to the townsfolk.

"Run! Get out!"

Davis hauled the boy up and shoved him forward, screaming at the others to move. Ivar watched through blurred vision as the townsfolk poured past him, Davis reaching down and grabbing Ivar under the arm, dragging him with surprising strength.

He twisted in time to see Bajurn still battling the drake, slashing at it with claws and broken timber, weaving between deadly strikes. The elder drake reared up, wings spreading wide like a storm about to break.

And then Bajurn disappeared into the smoke.

A roar tore through the air — a terrible, final sound — and then nothing.

Ivar screamed his friend’s name until his voice broke, but no answer came.

Davis and the townsfolk dragged him onward, away from the ruins, away from the monster.

Away from Bajurn.
