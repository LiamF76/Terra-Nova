
Ivar stumbled through the dark alone, the mud and roots beneath his feet trying time and time again to trip him. They eventually succeeded, and he collapsed into the dirt, no willpower left to save himself. He rolled over in the dirt, peering upwards at the night sky, watching the stars hang over him like droplets of silver, wondering if they were looking back at him.

His breath came in ragged, each inhale tasting of blood and ash, his wounds were not properly healed, and it was showing in his condition. The quiet of the night consumed him, and he felt the silence crawling up his skin like spiders. No screams, no roaring fires, no cackling goblins. Nothing but his breathing and the faint rustle of leaves in the cool air. The earth was cold and uncaring, but it held him up in a way his body couldn't.

His fingers twitched against the ground, remembering the warmth of Bajurn's blood on his palms. He could still feel the old Dragonborn's arm across his shoulder, the weight of his growing heavier with each step as they fled Rookswood until...

He swallowed hard. No.

Bajurn was gone. Torn from the world in a blaze of smoke and flame, facing down the elder drake so that Ivar, the boy who thought himself a man, could crawl away like a wounded animal.

His lips parted, but no sound came. Not a scream. Not a sob. Only the rasp of his breath against the stillness of the woods.

A hot tear slid into the corner of his mouth, tasting of ash.

*Why didn't I die too?*

A breeze whispered through the trees, carrying with it the faint scent of smoke and charred flesh. It made his stomach churn. His arms and legs ached as though weighted with iron, and yet some stubborn ember in his chest refused to let him lie still.

Bajurn’s words rang in his skull, faint but unyielding:

| "When you wake up, grab the nearest blade and get back in the fight."

Ivar exhaled shakily, the mist of his breath curling into the air. He didn’t feel like a fighter. He felt hollow, gutted, less a man than a shell.

But he forced himself to sit up.

His body protested, muscles screaming, wounds oozing sluggishly. His hand fell to the dagger still sheathed at his belt — Bajurn’s dagger, pressed into his grip in those final moments. The hilt felt foreign in his hand. Heavy.

He stared at it for a long time. Then he wiped the blood and mud from the blade on his sleeve and pushed himself to his feet.

The forest loomed around him, silent and watchful. Somewhere behind, the glow of Rookswood’s dying fires still stained the horizon a dull red. Ahead, only darkness.

Ivar squared his shoulders, though they trembled under the weight. He had nowhere to go. No one waiting for him.

But he walked anyway.

---

The village was nothing like Rookswood. Here, the houses were stone instead of timber, squat and huddled close together like they feared the wide plains beyond. Smoke curled lazily from a few chimneys, but the air stank of wet hay and unwashed bodies.

Ivar kept his head down as he walked through the market square, clutching Bajurn’s dagger tight under his cloak. The sun was high, but it felt no warmer than the dim light that had filtered through the forest. He was thinner now, his clothes loose on his frame, his face shadowed by weeks of dirt and exhaustion.

He didn’t plan to stay long. Just a night, enough to rest his legs and buy something that passed for food. Maybe bread, if there was any left.

But the sound of shouting drew his attention.

At the far end of the square, a man stood on a wagon, addressing a crowd. He wore a battered breastplate polished to a dull sheen and a crimson cloak thrown carelessly over one shoulder. His voice carried easily across the square:

"We can’t wait for others to save us. The goblins won’t stop at Rookswood. They won’t stop until every village burns."

Ivar froze mid-step. Rookswood.

The man swept a gauntleted hand across the villagers. His face was lined but handsome, with sharp eyes that seemed to measure everyone in the crowd. He was older, black hair with litters of silver beginning to creep from his scalp. He looked strong, he looked experienced, he looked dangerous.

"My name is Lord Ferran of Felldoor. Some of you may know mem some don't. One thing we all know is what's coming, not the King's armies, not the Hyperion Knights, but Goblins, in greater numbers even than at Rookswood. I need strong hands and stronger hearts. Join me. Fight with me. Take your land back."

He paused, taking in a deep breath before his next decree.

"If any of you are survivors of the attack at Rookswood, please come forward. We are looking for a boy, known to your village, he is called Ivar. You will be compensated if there is any information you can offer."

Ivar felt the blood drain from his face. He gripped the dagger tighter beneath his cloak, fingers slick with sweat. He wasn’t ready, but that hadn't stopped the Goblins from attacking his home, destroying everything he had known, and killing Bajurn. Fuck being ready, it was time to be, whether he was or not.

Ivar stepped forward from the crowd, pushing past bigger men, stronger men, people who belonged in the fight, unlike him. He tried to ignore the stares, and eventually he reached the foot of the platform.

Lord Ferran looked down at the boy, he was scrawny, starved, and quite dirty. Ferran’s sharp eyes fixed on him. They were like steel in the sun: cold, cutting, but not without a glint of something warmer beneath. His gauntleted fingers flexed against the pommel of his sword, not in threat but in thought.

"You from Rookswood boy?" He asked bluntly, leaning forward and meeting Ivar's curious dark eyes, familiar dark eyes...

"Yes." Ivar said, his knuckles white from gripping the hilt of his shoddy dagger. He held Ferran's gaze for a moment, suddenly so intense, like he was looking up at a great enemy, a foe whose will he needed to break, but it faded suddenly. Ivar shot his eyes down again, confused by that moment of anger that had gripped him so terribly.

Ferran was visibly surprised, his greying eyebrows raising before settling low on his brow again. He nodded at the boy, "You'll ride with me then."

He called out again for volunteers, receiving a few shouts from aspiring young warriors. Out in the Greenpeaks like Rookswood or this hamlet, people knew the dangers. Monsters, beasts, all manner of bandits and evils lay out in the wild hills. %Those boys wanted to do something about it, join the front and protect the lands of Escarth, Ivar just wanted to survive the night.

---

The road south wound like a sleeping serpent through golden fields, the snow of the Great Divide long melted into damp earth and bursting spring buds. The warmth was strange after so many months of cold; it clung to the travelers like an unwelcome second skin.

Ivar rode slightly apart from the others, his cloak pulled tight though the sun offered no reason for it. He had introduced himself at dawn, voice low, eyes downcast:

"Ivar."

Nothing more. And it seemed enough.

Garrick didn’t seem to notice, or care, about his silence. The young man filled the gaps himself, his booming voice riding the air like a warhorn.

"I’m telling you, Ferran’s got no idea how lucky he is to have me," Garrick said, laughing so hard his horse flicked its ears back in protest. "Before the Greenpeak Goblins are dead, he’ll have me knighted. Sir Garrick of Felldoor! Gods, it sounds right, doesn’t it?"

Ferran, riding at the head of the small column, only chuckled, his deep, rumbling voice carrying back. “If you’re half as good with a blade as you are with your jaw, lad, the goblins don’t stand a chance.”

"Just you lot wait and see!" Garrick thumped his chest with a gloved fist. His confidence was infectious, even Ivar caught himself smirking faintly. But he saw through it too. Garrick’s laugh was too loud, his boasts too eager. The boy was scared. Just like all of them.

The sound of hooves approached from his right. Ivar’s fingers twitched near the dagger hidden beneath his cloak. Reflex. A habit born in Rookswood’s ashes.

"Mind if I ride with you?"

He looked up to see the dragonborn volunteer—a brass-scaled figure gleaming like a knight in molten armor. The man’s horns curved forward like a demon’s, but his golden eyes were impossibly soft, almost beautiful in the midday light.

"You said your name was Ivar?" The Dragonborn’s voice was smooth, calm, as if they were discussing the weather. He leaned forward slightly, gloved hand stroking his horse’s neck.

Ivar nodded once. "I did."

"I’m Tharion." The dragonborn smiled, not the fanged grin Ivar expected, but a small, reassuring one. "Don’t mind Garrick. He’ll talk your ears off if you let him."

"I’ve noticed."

That earned a quiet chuckle from Tharion. "You’re not much for talking yourself, are you?"

"Not today."

"Good. I prefer a peaceful ride." He shifted slightly in the saddle, letting silence settle between them—not uncomfortable, but companionable, like two travelers sharing a road without needing to fill it with words. "You're from Rookswood. Must have been a bloody attack. I'm sorry for that, we're going to rout those monsters once and for all."

Ivar nodded solemnly, but did not reply.

---

The group rode in a loose column now, the road flattening as it ran beside wide meadows dotted with the first flowers of spring. Birds chattered in the hedgerows. A pair of foxes darted across the path and vanished into the undergrowth.

Ferran’s deep voice broke the rhythm of hoofbeats and creaking leather. "Well boys, I suppose you'll want to know just what you're getting into, but I have a test for you first. You're patrolling my lands, and on horseback you and your band spot a Goblin troupe raiding a nearby village. What do you do?"

Garrick laughed uproariously, as if the very idea of the question amused him. "I’d hit them hard and fast, Lord Ferran. Show them what happens when they come near our homes."

Tharion tilted his head, his brass scales catching the sun like stained glass windows in a chapel. "But a strike like that risks survivors. They’ll regroup and retaliate. I’d track them back—cut off the snake at the head."
