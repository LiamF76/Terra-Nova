We crest the grassy hill at dawn, my favorite time of day. The morning dew on the blades of grass, the sun barely lighting the world into a perfect twilight. It is a peaceful time, between the horrors of night and labors of day. I always feel uniquely calm while the rest of the world is sleeping.

"Beautiful morning. Still quite a ways to go." Lukas said as he wiped the coffee out of his mustache. He handed me the drink and I took a small sip, we had enough left to kickstart us the next morning, and no more. Hopefully we could find work that pays well enough to buy more, Lukas treasures prayer to Tena over morning coffee.

It's chilly out, and I would rather be beside the fire, or walking to keep warm, but Lukas has us huddled behind trees on the ridge, waiting. The faint chuff of a steed sets him at attention, and he whistles at me, pointing beyond the tree line.

There I see our target, the thing Lukas has had us chasing since he saw it. A gorgeous chestnut colored horse, tall and strong, a beautiful steed. By Lukas' eye, a trained war horse. Her hooves carried old iron horseshoes, and her pelt looked disheveled, like a mount abandoned, not a wild horse. We had noticer her in the plains days ago, and had been chasing ever since, Lukas couldn't tolerate walking any longer.

I had offered to let him ride Pollux, but my magical friend did not take kindly to being ridden by Lukas, I was too afraid to try. 

As we waited in the brush, Lukas pulled the lasso he had fashioned off his shoulder, holding it with two hands. We just had to wait for the beauty to get close enough. It pranced through the tall grasses, falling right into my trap. A small bushel of sweet flower, horses loved them. I used to pick them and feed the caravan horses when they were nervous, a treat always calmed the 
poor things. It took a bite, and we launched into attack.

Lukas leapt up, and I did as well. I threw my arms up and shouted, trying to drive it towards Lukas, he was silent, but tossed the lasso. it was a perfect throw, landing gently around her neck, tightened, and we had her. It was harrowing, she almost escaped... if she had been trying to run.

She just stood there, looked at Lukas who had just lassoed her, and went back to eating the flowers. 

I furrowed my brow and looked at Lukas. "I suppose we never checked if she was friendly."

He approaches cautiously, gently petting the war horse's snout. "Oh she is very nice, probably could've walked right up to her and thrown a saddle on." He gripped the lasso, and gently pulled the horse along behind him to a clear we had made out camp, she followed without protest. "There's a good girl. Well done Inayet! Now it is time to get you on your horse."
