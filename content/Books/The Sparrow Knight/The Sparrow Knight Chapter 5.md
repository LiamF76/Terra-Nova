The thunder outside is like someone banging on the outside of my skull.

I wake up gasping, my body is wrapped in linens that smell faintly of lavender and the old stones of Tarnkeep. For a moment I don't know where I am, only that the room is near to a window, because I can smell thew rain and hear the thunder outside. As I manage to sit myself up in the bed, I can see a narrow window, the rain lashing against it with force like skittering fingers, a bolt of lightning flashes past outside.

A faint rumble goes through the bones of the castle, the faded tapestries and banners in my room flutter, and the small heart fire flickers. It is quaint, but warm enough to satisfy my recovering body.

I twist and stretch, pain radiating up my right arm in a slow grinding wave, like stone against stone. It's a deep... wrong, kind of pain. It's heavy, like the ache itself weighs twenty pounds. 

I bite back a cry and turn my head, dread settling in my chest before I even look at the wound. My entire right hand and forearm are wrapped in bandages. The fingers beneath barely move when I will it, the skin is stiff where it should be soft, like a hard outer shell. The flesh within feels numb and weak, like it has been melted to liquid. It's all wrong.

Instead of motion, of a strong grip to hold my sword or a soft hand to shake that of a friend, there is deep pressure, a chill seeping inward followed by a pulse of searing pain that makes my vision blur.

I vaguely remembered the physician saying something of 'necrotic', but it mattered not. Whatever it was, both my and Lukas' wounds were beyond treatment, at least to any of Tarn's healers.

We would need to visit a great temple for something this dark. It was ancient magic, worse than necrotic, Alcor believed it to be a curse.

Another crack of thunder rattles the shutters outside, the candle on my bedside table is faint, nearly snuffed out by a draft of wind within the castle. It smells of rain and age.

Shadows leap across the walls as the lights dance in my hearth, and I hear footsteps outside. I carefully twist myself to sit on the edge of the iron bed frame, which groans in. protest. I cradle my injured hand with the other, afraid to touch too firmly, afraid not to.

The pain keeps me anchored to the present, to this storming moment, to the simple fact that I am awake, and that something in me is dying while the thunder rolls on outside.

A knock on my door breaks the internal silence, and it feels as if time had been frozen, suddenly resumed by the presence of another.

I bid them come in, and am greeted by the face of Lord Tarn. I try to mask my surprise, but he smiles knowingly, clearly my attempt at acting nonchalant had not gone unnoticed.

He takes a seat beside the small drawing table in a sturdy wooden chair, placing his elbows on his knees, and rubbing his temples gently. "I apologize for turning up unannounced. I was speaking with your companion, Mr. Petlia, and he asked me to see you immediately. I would like to discuss your injury."

He holds his hands out, asking me to present my wound, I hold out my right arm, allowing him to inspect. 

He slowly unwraps the bandages, inspecting the necrosis, the dying blackened flesh, dark even for my tanned complexion. "I sent you into that battle, and for this I apologize. I gave double you and Petlia's going rate as payment, but the least I could do for both of you is... anything in my power for these injuries. You were lucky though you know." He says as he finishes unwrapping my arm. He taps the ring on my hand. I hadn't seen it since I woke up yesterday.

The entire finger the ring rests on is unharmed, and it seems to have abated the infection along the rest of my arm somewhat as well. "Whatever this is, it is a mighty magic, and it may have saved you. This infection would have taken your sword arm Inayet. Luckily the ring protected you, and I may be able to offer some more help."

I nod cautiously. "Looks like my ring here did that for you."

He chuckles in a highborn accent, how does a laugh even have an accent? Its damned regal, like I made a joke about gold trading rates and frontier politics. 

"It saved you from losing your arm, but the infection will still spread through the blood. We must stop the flow, and you are lucky a Tarn is here to do it."

His eyes meet mine, and a spectral glint falls over them, like light passing through a crystal. "My family is a sorcerous bloodline, our ancestry is magic, there are few left with this power. We are born of the great basilisk, Thauxxos. I can turn, " he says, running his finger gently along the upper limit of my curse, just below the elbow joint. "all of this, to stone. Enchanted stone. You will be able to move it, it will be just as it was, perhaps stronger."

I raise an eyebrow at the offer, "What's the catch then?" Knowing this is too good to be true.

"Well, I can only do it once, but it should last you quite a while, could be months, could be years. Though the catch is, as you no doubt expected, this will not cure you, it will stop the spread until the stone softens, then you will be dying again."

"I thought basilisk could turn men to statues, if my hand is stone I'll barely be able to lift my hand, not work, certainly not fight."

"Ah but Inayet, we have had this power for many years. We have learned its secrets. Once we would turn entire armies to stone, men enchanted with skin so strong, no blade could cut it, no spear could pierce it. My grandfather spoke of Tarn Knights who wrestled venomous green dragons bare handed using nothing but the power of our blood. You would be stronger, a force of nature bonded to earth and magic."

He seems to want to go on, he's pitching it to me, and then it dawns on me: He's never done this. It's his family's magic, not his, and he is now relishing someone desperate and expendable enough to test it on. I don't care what he gets out of it, but I do care that there is a chance for one of us. "Then do it to Lukas. Save him first. Don't take no for an answer, he's a stubborn old goat."

Tarn sighs, and places a hand on my shoulder, bad news. "I am sorry Mr. Sparrow. The wound in Lukas' side may have been saved, had it not already spread to his heart. Were I to turn that to stone, he would surely die. This magic cannot save him, but for you, there is a chance."

The infection is trapped in my hand, but my whole body feels numb. Damn it all.

---

I don't remember much of the strange ritual they performed, only that it was important I stayed conscious. I didn't. Once they opened up my arm, had Lord Tarn stare at me a bit too deeply for me to keep eye contact, and start literally pouring his (somehow green) saliva into the wound, I passed out from the shock and pain.

When I awoke however, the pressure, the weight, and that necromantic cold, were gone. They were replaced with something else, a strength, like my two feet had been molded so firmly with the earth not even Balthazaar could knock me down.
