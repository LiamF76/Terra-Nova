The thunder outside is like someone banging on the outside of my skull.

I wake up gasping, my body is wrapped in linens that smell faintly of lavender and the old stones of Tarnkeep. For a moment I don't know where I am, only that the room is near to a window, because I can smell thew rain and hear the thunder outside. As I manage to sit myself up in the bed, I can see a narrow window, the rain lashing against it with force like skittering fingers, a bolt of lightning flashes past outside.

A faint rumble goes through the bones of the castle, the faded tapestries and banners in my room flutter, and the small heart fire flickers. It is quaint, but warm enough to satisfy my recovering body.

I twist and stretch, pain radiating up my right arm in a slow grinding wave, like stone against stone. It's a deep... wrong, kind of pain. It's heavy, like the ache itself weighs twenty pounds. 

I bite back a cry and turn my head, dread settling in my chest before I even look at the wound. My entire right hand and forearm are wrapped in bandages. The fingers beneath barely move when I will it, the skin is stiff where it should be soft, like a hard outer shell. The flesh within feels numb and weak, like it has been melted to liquid. It's all wrong.

Instead of motion, of a strong grip to hold my sword or a soft hand to shake that of a friend, there is deep pressure, a chill seeping inward followed by a pulse of searing pain that makes my vision blur.

I vaguely remembered the physician saying something of 'necrotic', but it mattered not. Whatever it was, both my and Lukas' wounds were beyond treatment, at least to any of Tarn's healers.

We would need to visit a great temple for something this dark. It was ancient magic, worse than necrotic, Alcor believed it to be a curse.

Another crack of thunder rattles the shutters outside, the candle on my bedside table is faint, nearly snuffed out by a draft of wind within the castle. It smells of rain and age.
