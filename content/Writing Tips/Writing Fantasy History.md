# Ancient History

- no eyewitnesses, no records, those that exist are incomplete, distorted, and lost
- this is where the foundations can be introduced, great civilizations, cosmic events, cataclysmic events
- myths don't have to be super detailed, less detail can make them more interesting
- artifacts / traditions that are still passed down today
- players will fill in gaps with their imagination
- elements that create awe and curiosity
- EX: pyramids of Egypt, people still don't understand everything about them, speculative on how they were built. Unclear origins

---

# Recent History

- some people may still be alive (could be 200+ years ago, because of fantasy)
- better documented but still subject to interpretation but factions, places, and governments
- explore conflicts and alliances that shape the current world, perhaps people who are veterans or were occupied and invaded
- show how it impacts the present, failed revolution leaves behind shattered kingdom, natural disaster that displaced populations

---

# Modern History

- Immediate present, the layer players interact with most
- current events, political climate good or bad, thinking about everyday life
- where the past and present come together to shape challenges and conflicts of today
- should show distant and recent past
- EX: rival factions of today that are rivals because of recent past fighting over ancient ruins
